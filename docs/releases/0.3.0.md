# tscache 0.3.0 (in preparation)

Not released yet: these notes collect what the next release adds, step by
step, until it is cut.

```sh
npm install @anishbenji/tscache@~0.3.0
```

## Highlights

- **Memory budgets.** `createClient({ memoryBudget })` and
  `new Engine({ memoryBudget })` bound, in bytes, what a worker's caches hold:
  in a SharedWorker every tab's caches together. Above the budget the worker
  evicts the least recently used pages of any cache, with their coverage, so
  a later read fetches an evicted range again and never mistakes it for a
  confirmed gap. Reads and writes in any tab keep a page recent; the pages a
  pending `get` is reading and those holding a provisional bar are never
  evicted, so the budget may be exceeded while they are held. Without a
  budget nothing changes: nothing is counted or evicted
  ([Memory](https://anishbenji.github.io/tscache/guides/patterns#memory)).
- **`memoryStats()`.** `client.memoryStats()` and `engine.memoryStats()`
  report the budget in force, the bytes the cached points hold, the pinned
  part, the pages evicted so far and one row per cache.
- **Eviction in change events.** `cacheChanged` gains an optional `evicted`
  list: coverage the budget evicted, which a view does not need to read again.
  With a budget, a later `invalidate` or watermark moved back over an evicted
  range still lists it in `removed`, so a view showing it hears the change
  ([Change events](https://anishbenji.github.io/tscache/concepts/events#change-events)).
- **Persistence follows memory.** With a budget, an evicted page's record is
  deleted at the next write-behind flush, so the disk and the next session's
  load stay within what the budget let the worker hold; an evicted range is
  fetched again in the next session too.

- **Calendar intervals.** A cache's `interval` may be a calendar interval
  in place of a number of milliseconds: one slot per day, week, month,
  quarter or year in an IANA time zone, starting at a local time.
  `{ unit: "day", zone: "America/New_York", at: "09:30" }` holds daily bars
  stamped at the session's open all year, across daylight saving time, and
  `{ unit: "month" }` holds monthly bars at their real instants, where the
  earlier recipes stored dates or ordinals. Ranges, timestamps, coverage,
  misses, watermarks and events stay milliseconds. Every calendar day is a
  slot, so a weekend or a holiday is an empty slot that a fetch confirms as
  a gap. Zone rules come from the runtime's `Intl`; nothing is bundled
  ([Calendar intervals](https://anishbenji.github.io/tscache/concepts/grid#calendar-intervals)).
- **`@anishbenji/tscache/grid`.** A new entry with `snapRange`, `slotStart`
  and `nextSlotStart`: the grid arithmetic the cache itself uses, for fixed
  and calendar intervals, for a view that needs the range a read returns,
  the bar forming now or the start of the next one.
- **Snapshots and records of calendar caches.** A calendar cache writes
  `format: 2` snapshots, payloads and stored records, which name the grid as
  `grid` and store the slot index of each instant that names a slot. A
  reader whose time-zone data places one of those instants in another slot
  (a browser update, a server and its page) refuses the snapshot or skips
  the stored page and fetches again, so a bar is never filed under another
  day. Fixed caches keep writing `format: 1`, byte for byte.

## Compatibility

- The protocol stays at version 1: the `memoryBudget` and `memoryStats` ops
  are additive, and the worker lists `'memoryBudget'` in its hello's
  `features` (architecture N101). A client facing a worker script from before
  them sends no budget, warns once in a development build, and its
  `memoryStats()` rejects with `TscacheError`.
- In a SharedWorker the first budget the worker receives holds until it
  restarts, and a budget that reaches a worker already holding a cache is not
  applied (N88, N103); a development build warns in both cases.
- `TscacheClient` gains `memoryStats()`, `ClientOptions` gains `memoryBudget`,
  `WorkerFeature` gains `'memoryBudget'`, and `cacheChanged` gains the optional
  `evicted`. Code that implements `TscacheClient` itself, such as a test
  double, must add `memoryStats`. `./engine` exports `EngineOptions` and both
  entries export `MemoryStats`.
- Brotli sizes grow: `index` 5.00 to 5.31 kB, the in-process hosting 12.72 to
  14.25 kB, `worker` 13.98 to 15.50 kB, `engine` 10.11 to 11.57 kB.

- **Compile-time breaks from calendar intervals,** which widen types whatever
  grid a cache uses; nothing changes at runtime for a fixed interval:
  - `CacheConfig.interval`, `ResolvedCacheConfig.interval` (returned by
    `Engine.cache()` and `configOf()`) and `FetchRequest.interval` are
    `number | CalendarInterval` (the resolved ones
    `number | ResolvedCalendarInterval`). Arithmetic on `interval` no
    longer compiles: narrow it first, `typeof interval === "number"`.
  - `Snapshot`, `DenseSegmentPayload`, `EncodedSnapshot` and
    `EncodedSegmentPayload` are unions of their format 1 and format 2 shapes.
    Code that reads their `interval` or `alignmentOffset` narrows on `format`
    first. `FixedSnapshot`, `CalendarSnapshot`, `FixedSegmentPayload` and
    `CalendarSegmentPayload` name the members.
  - **Not caught by the compiler:** a fetcher that formats the interval as a
    string, `String(request.interval)` or a template literal, compiles and
    sends `[object Object]` for a calendar cache. Narrow it and name the unit
    yourself (the fetcher guide shows how).
- A calendar interval needs a worker that supports it: such a worker lists
  `'calendar-grid'` in its hello's `features`, and against a worker script
  from before it `client.cache()` rejects a calendar config with
  `ConfigError`, before anything is sent. The protocol stays at version 1.
- A 0.2.0 engine and a 0.2.0 `decodeSnapshot` refuse a calendar cache's
  `format: 2` snapshots and records as an unsupported format; after a
  rollback the older version rewrites its own records.
- `decodeSnapshot` no longer quotes an input that is not an object in its
  error: the message names the input's type only, as in
  `invalid encoded snapshot: must be an object, got a string`. A snapshot
  that arrives as an unparsed string used to be printed whole, so an
  application that logged the message logged the snapshot.
- `hydrate`'s errors no longer print a value they refuse, except a format of
  1 or 2: most name the refused field and the type it received, finite
  numbers included, as in
  `invalid snapshot: finalizedUntil must be a number, got a string`, and the
  rest name only what was expected. A few still print, as context, a
  timestamp or slot index the snapshot has already passed validation for,
  so an application should log the error's name only. Several messages are reworded (`unsupported format 3` is now
  `format must be 1 or 2, got a finite number`); code that matched their old
  text should match the prefixes `invalid snapshot:` and
  `invalid segment payload:` instead.
- Moving a cache from a fixed interval to a calendar interval is a structural
  change: use a new cache id, as for any change of structure.
- `WorkerFeature` gains `'calendar-grid'`; the root, `./engine` and `./fetcher`
  entries export `CalendarInterval` and `ResolvedCalendarInterval`.
- Brotli sizes grow with the calendar grid: `index` 5.31 to 5.65 kB, the
  in-process hosting 14.25 to 17.66 kB, `worker` 15.50 to 18.90 kB, `engine`
  11.57 to 15.17 kB; the new `grid` entry is 3.64 kB.

## Known limits

- The budget counts the bytes the cached points' arrays have allocated, not
  the worker's whole footprint: object overhead and transient copies (a read's
  result on its way to the page, a load's records while they are applied) are
  not counted.
- Evicted history is fetched again from the backend rather than reloaded from
  the disk; loading evicted pages from IndexedDB on demand needs lazy loading,
  a later design step.
- A tab of an older deploy without the option that opened a cache first keeps
  the SharedWorker unbudgeted until it restarts (finding M5).
- Pages that hold coverage but no bar (confirmed gaps) are not counted and
  never evicted.
- Calendar intervals: units below a day are not supported. A cache's
  time-zone rules are the runtime's. A snapshot or stored page is checked at
  the instants that name a slot, not at every bar: when two runtimes disagree
  only about dates strictly inside it (a daylight-saving rule one of them
  lacks, with both ends in the season where they agree), it applies, and the
  reader shows those bars at its own start of their slots. Two aliases of one
  zone name two caches. A calendar cache in a zone refuses a snapshot, and
  loads only the newest stored pages, beyond about 14,500 separate coverage
  ranges.
