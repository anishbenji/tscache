# N95: What eviction does to a persisted page's record

Row N95 of the decision table in `docs/architecture.md` §8, whose introduction says how its rows were taken. Recorded in step 121 from the M3 design proposal (`docs/design/m3-memory.md`). Decisions are locked (`AGENTS.md`); the architecture section lists every decision with a one-line summary.

## Resolution

**Eviction marks the page as a write does, and the next flush deletes its record, relaxed.** It is not a removal: it does not make the flush strict, adds nothing to the removal count, and no call waits for it (N58 unchanged). After a flush the disk holds `recordsOf(S)`, as §4.10.2 and §4.10.6 property (a) say, and N46, N57 and N58 are untouched; the segment bytes on disk and those of one cache's eager load are bounded by what memory held at the cache's last flush. If the deletion fails until its deadline the cache stops (N59) and the stop's cleanup deletes its records; only when the cleanup fails too, or the worker closes first, does the record stay, and a later worker loads it: its data was right when the page was evicted. The cost: evicted history is fetched again from the network, in this session and the next. Rows E10 (dirty pages pinned until flushed) and E12 (a full rewrite keeps evicted records) are moot with this option. Rejected for now: keeping the record and loading it on demand (needs lazy loading, which amends N46 and N57, and a disk budget; a later design step) and keeping it for the next worker's eager load (unbounded disk, and an eager load beyond the budget). This amends the §4.10.9 eviction row. User decision 67, row E11 option (b), 2026-11-16 (§4.10.9, step 121)
