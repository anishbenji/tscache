# N61: The writer and its one deadline

Row N61 of the decision table in `docs/architecture.md` §8, whose introduction says how its rows were taken. The text below is that row, moved here unchanged in step 73. Decisions are locked (`AGENTS.md`); the architecture section lists every decision with a one-line summary.

## Resolution

**One writer per persister runs every write transaction (a flush, a `clearAll`, a stop's cleanup) as a job with one deadline, `timeoutMs` after it is due; jobs carrying a removal run before queued jobs that carry none; a store call still pending at the deadline is abandoned; at the deadline the job stops its cache (or, for `clearAll`, every tracked cache).** Queue wait, store calls and retries count against the one deadline, so every job settles within `timeoutMs` of being due and a removal's call waits at most two deadlines (the job and the cleanup). A retry is a request coalesced into the cache's one queued job, which keeps the earliest deadline of what it absorbed, so a failed addition job and a removal that arrives meanwhile become one job bounded by the earlier deadline (round 3). Added by the redesign after review round 2, as the mechanism behind N58 and N59. Weighed and rejected: one writer per cache (a removal would still wait behind the running job of its cache; a single queue with priority needs no second mechanism); a deadline per attempt with a retry count (what round 2 found unbounded); per-cache deletions after a failed `clearAll` (a disk that refused the clear for a deadline refuses them too, and they would cost one deadline per cache). Redesign, 2026-10-30. The Web Lock the first form requested for each job, under an `AbortSignal` that fired at the deadline, was retired 2026-10-31 (user decision #8: SharedWorker only): the writer is its database's only writer (N52). **Changed 2026-10-31 (design round 6, R6-1):** a `clearAll` job is also a barrier. From its call until it settles, through every retry, no flush job starts. The job running at the call finishes its attempt, which is final: a rejection or a pending store call at its deadline ends the job without a retry or a stop, and its taken state is not put back, since the clear holds it. Queued flush jobs, retries included, are absorbed into the clear; cleanups queued before the call keep their place ahead of it; a request made during the barrier becomes due at the later of its own due time and the settling, with its deadline `timeoutMs` after that, so no job is starved into a stop by time spent behind the barrier. A flush attempt that finds no store opens it, which happens only after a `clearAll` found no database. Every job still settles within `timeoutMs` of being due, and a removal's call still waits at most two deadlines (N58). **Clarified 2026-10-31 (step 38, lazy loading, §4.10.8):** a load's deadline counts from the hosting's `open`, the persistence chunk's import included. A removal on a cache whose open waits for the chunk, and a `clearAll` before the chunk has loaded, first wait for the import, itself bounded by `timeoutMs`, and then for at most the two deadlines above. Round 6 found that ordering removal-carrying jobs by due time let a flush due before a failed clear's retry run first; the retry then deleted what it wrote. Rejected: priority ordering alone (R6-1); giving the clear precedence over every other job (between two attempts the clear is not due, so a flush due in that gap still runs first).
