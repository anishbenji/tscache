# N59: Persistence failures

Row N59 of the decision table in `docs/architecture.md` §8, whose introduction says how its rows were taken. The text below is that row, moved here unchanged in step 73. Decisions are locked (`AGENTS.md`); the architecture section lists every decision with a one-line summary.

## Resolution

**Never a rejected call.** The store failing to open, or a load beyond 5 s, stops the cache with `'unavailable'`; a failed read with `'error'`; a quota error stops it at once (N47). A failed write is retried after a second with the union of the dirty state, until the job's deadline (N61): then the cache stops, with `'error'` and the last error when an attempt was rejected, or `'unavailable'` when none settled. A stopped cache's records are deleted with one strict write under its own deadline, since the disk may cover what memory withdrew; when that deletion fails as well, the records stay, the one way a removal can outlive its call. Rejected: retrying forever (a silent failure); stopping on the first transient failure. Design, 2026-10-30; user-confirmed 2026-10-30. **Changed in substance after review round 2:** the first design bounded each store call at 5 s, retried three times 5 s apart and gave lock acquisition no bound, so the "bounded" wait of a removal was the sum of an unbounded number of bounded parts (round 2, R2-2). Now one deadline per job of the writer, `timeoutMs` (5 s) from the moment it is due, covers queue, lock, store calls and retries together; the retry count and the per-call timeout are gone. The 5 s bound on `cache()` (N46) is the same option. Redesign, 2026-10-30. **Clarified 2026-10-31 (step 38, lazy loading, §4.10.8):** in the SharedWorker the persister is a chunk loaded at the first persisted open or `clearAll`; a chunk that fails to load, or does not load within the load's 5 s, stops every waiting and later open with `'unavailable'` and turns persistence off for the worker's life, as a store that fails to open does.

## Amendment, 2026-11-21

User decision 95 (option B of `docs/reviews/step-132.md`), changing this row in substance for one case: a stop that ends a cache's load, at the load's deadline or on a rejected read, has no cleanup. The records stay, and a later session loads them. A loading cache has changed nothing (N57: any mutation abandons the load first), so the disk cannot cover anything memory withdrew, which is the reason the cleanup exists; deleting the records only lost the device's history to a stalled or briefly failing disk. Every other stop keeps its cleanup: a write's deadline, a quota error, and a `restore` that throws after a successful read. A failed `clearAll` still stops its caches without a per-cache cleanup (N61). Weighed and rejected: keeping the deletion (a transient stall wipes the history); a bounded number of failed loads across sessions before a cleanup (a persisted counter, a record-format change, and a write from a stopped cache); cleaning up after a rejected read only (IndexedDB's errors do not tell a corrupt store from a passing fault). Cost accepted: a cache whose load fails in every session stays cold and keeps its records until a `clearAll`, a load that succeeds, or the browser evicts them. Architecture §4.10.1 and §4.10.6 state the rule.

## Amendment, 2026-11-21 (user decision 113 A)

User decision 113 A (step 132, `docs/reviews/step-132.md`), amending the amendment above and keeping N58. Pre-review 2 of step 132 found that a removal on a cache stopped by its load was acknowledged while the disk still covered the removed range, so a later session loaded data the application had removed: the stopped cache counted no removal, and its `durable` calls were released at once. Now a cache stopped by its load (the load's deadline, a rejected read, a store that fails to open, also for a cache opened after it failed, and in the SharedWorker a persistence chunk that fails to import or does not import in time) keeps its records under decision 95, and its first later removal queues the strict cleanup the stop deferred; the removal's call, and every removal of the cache until that cleanup settles, waits for it. The cleanup is the one of this row: due at once, one deadline (`timeoutMs`), no retry, its failure ignored, the records staying when it fails. When no store is open it opens one that exists. Without the chunk, the SharedWorker hosting runs the deletion through its store opener, which in the worker imports the same chunk: when that import fails again, the deletion fails under the same rule. A stop that ends the load with no later removal still deletes nothing. A cleanup that a `clearAll`'s own clear queues is absorbed by that clear. This also closes the store-open and chunk-import cases, which predate decision 95 and released the removal at once as well. Architecture §4.10.1, §4.10.6, §4.10.8 and §4.10.9 state the rule.
