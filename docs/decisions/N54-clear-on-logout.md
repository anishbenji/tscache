# N54: Clearing on logout (P10)

Row N54 of the decision table in `docs/architecture.md` §8, whose introduction says how its rows were taken. The text below is that row, moved here unchanged in step 73. Decisions are locked (`AGENTS.md`); the architecture section lists every decision with a one-line summary.

## Resolution

**`clearAll` empties the disk**: both stores, the caches this engine never opened included, with strict durability (N49), and the call resolves after. In the SharedWorker hosting that engine serves every tab, so one call clears memory and disk for all of them; a SharedWorker of another deploy may write its own state back (§4.10.7). The sentence "With one engine per tab another tab writes its own state back, so a logout clears every tab" was retired 2026-10-31 (user decision #8: SharedWorker only): those hostings no longer touch the disk, so their `clearAll` clears their memory only. That last clause was in turn changed on 2026-11-02 (user decision #11 (1), option (b)): their `clearAll` empties the disk with a clear-only call (N66). Rejected: a separate `purge`. Director, 2026-10-30. **Changed 2026-10-31 (design round 6, R6-1):** the `clearAll` job is a barrier for the writer (§4.10.6). From the call until the job settles, through every retry, no flush is written and no load reads the disk, for any cache: the job running at the call makes a final attempt; queued flushes are absorbed into the clear; changes made meanwhile are held and flushed after it; a cache opened meanwhile starts cold and is rewritten after it (round 5's rule for R5-1, folded in); a second `clearAll()` joins the job and keeps its deadline. A clear that succeeds makes every removal recorded until it settled durable. A clear that fails stops every tracked cache, those opened during the barrier included, and the persister then persists nothing for its life: a cache opened later stops at once and reads nothing, so data a failed logout left on disk is not served back by the same worker. Round 5 found a load reading what a pending clear was about to delete, and round 6 a flush landing before the clear's retry, which then deleted it; both are the disk being used while a clear is pending, and the barrier excludes every such use. Rejected: ordering the jobs by priority alone (R6-1); letting flushes run during a pending clear and rewriting their caches after it succeeds (a second write of the same data, and the load race needs a rule of its own); later opens loading after a failed clear (serves what the logout was to delete); restarting the deadline at a second call (repeated calls could hold every flush indefinitely).
