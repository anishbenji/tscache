# N92: Which pages go first, and what counts as a use

Row N92 of the decision table in `docs/architecture.md` §8, whose introduction says how its rows were taken. Recorded in step 121 from the M3 design proposal (`docs/design/m3-memory.md`). Decisions are locked (`AGENTS.md`); the architecture section lists every decision with a one-line summary.

## Resolution

**Least recently used, where reads and writes of points are uses.** A use is a read of points (`get`, orchestrated or `cacheOnly`, and a snapshot), for every page the request overlaps that holds points, and a write of points (`put`, an orchestrated fetch's put, `hydrate` and `restore`), for every page the call wrote to: stored or replaced points in (a replace that empties a page writes it, and the page then leaves the order), never a page the call merely spans or that a failed write did not reach (clarified in step 121 after Codex rounds 2–3; the store reports each page where it writes). `coverage()`, `invalidate`, `setFinalizedUntil`, `memoryStats()` and the orchestrator's coverage probes are not uses. The order is a counter, not a clock. The user accepted the decision concern recorded with the row: a tab that opens a large persisted cache pushes its unread history ahead of the pages another tab shows, and that tab then fetches them again on its next read. Rejected: reads only (a page just put is the first to go) and restored pages entering at the least recent end (the opening tab would fetch from the network what the disk had just given it).
