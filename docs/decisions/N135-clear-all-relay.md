# N135: Whether `clearAll` reaches other workers

Row N135 of the decision table in `docs/architecture.md` §8, whose introduction says how its rows were taken. Recorded in step 135 from the M5 design proposal (`docs/design/m5-deploy-skew.md`), which keeps the alternatives in full. Decisions are locked (`AGENTS.md`); the architecture section lists every decision with a one-line summary.

## Resolution

**Yes, by a relay on the workers' channel.** Every `clearAll` carries a clear id, chosen where the call was made. The originator posts `{ t: 'clearAll', v: 1, id, clear }` twice: in the synchronous step that clears its memory, and when its own disk clear has settled, whether it succeeded, failed or reached its deadline. A SharedWorker that receives a message from another worker runs `clearAll` as if one of its own tabs had called it, for each message it receives. A worker that clears for a clear id it did not originate posts once, with that id, when its first disk clear for that id settles, and never again for that id; it keeps a small set of the ids it has seen. The dedicated-worker and in-process hostings post too, opening the channel at the call and closing it after their second post. The calling tab's promise does not wait for the other workers. The relay closes the logout gap N54 and N66 record for another SharedWorker, a worker that starts while the clear is pending, a lagging writer and chains of late workers included (proven by contract test 10; this claim is withdrawn by the amendment of 2026-11-26); a failed clear, workers of 0.1.0 and 0.2.0, a realm without `BroadcastChannel` and a hosting without IndexedDB remain. Cost: with k other workers each worker clears about k + 1 times per call. This extends the reach N54 and N66 give `clearAll`; the user approved their amendment and §4.10.7's explicitly. Rejected: two posts without a relay (narrows the gap but does not close it: a worker that flushed before handling the posts leaves records a later worker loads and keeps); only from SharedWorkers (leaves the dedicated and in-process gap); not at all. User decision 71, row F11 option (b), 2026-11-16 (§2.7, §4.10.7, §4.10.8, step 135)

## Amendment, 2026-11-21

User decision 2026-11-21 (#120): the set of seen clear ids is kept for the worker's life. "A small set" above is small in practice, one id per `clearAll` the worker hears, but it is not bounded: a bound by count let the clears circulate among workers for their lives (gate round 1, R1-1, `docs/reviews/step-135.md`), so a worker never forgets an id it has seen. The relay, its message and its two posts are unchanged.

## Amendment, 2026-11-26

The relay does not cover every chain of late workers. Gate round 10 of step 135 found a counterexample, reproduced with the repository's persistence classes on the channel of the deploy-skew persistence tests (`docs/reviews/step-135.md`, round 10). A worker that has already relayed a clear id first-opens a persisted cache, loads a lagging worker's late flush and writes the page back after the laggard's clear has deleted it, by an ordinary write. Its next clear for that id posts nothing, since the id is seen, so a newer worker that loaded the write-back never hears a clear and keeps serving pre-logout data until the next `clearAll`. Three workers of 0.3.0 or later suffice: the laggard, the cleared worker that first-opens and writes inside the laggard's window, and a newer worker that starts in the same window. The argument of §4.10.7 assumed that a worker which has cleared never holds pre-logout data again; this case breaks that assumption.

The Resolution's claim that the relay closes the gap for "chains of late workers included", which it said contract test 10 proves, is withdrawn. Step 152 adds a logout fence before 0.3.0 is released (decision #151 D, 2026-11-26); until it merges the case is a known limit (§4.10.7, What remains), stated in the persistence guide and the 0.3.0 release notes. The relay, its message, its two posts and the set of seen ids are unchanged.
