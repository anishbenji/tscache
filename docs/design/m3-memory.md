Status: **proposal for the user's decision** (step 98, 2026-11-14). Nothing here is built or approved. It answers finding M3 of the review of 2026-11-10 (`docs/reviews/2026-11-review.md`, M3 and §3c) under user decision 46 A: after 0.1.0, three design steps in the order M7, M3, M5, each a spec first, then the user's decision rows, then contract tests. ## 1. The gap

Nothing bounds the memory a cache holds, and nothing reports it. No eviction, budget or size call exists in `packages/tscache/src`; the public surface is `CacheHandle` (`get`, `put`, `hydrate`, `invalidate`, `clear`, `setFinalizedUntil`, `on`) and the client's `cache`, `clearAll`, `updateAuth`, `on`, `off`, `dispose` (`client/cache.ts:41-107`, `client/client.ts:43-71`). One year of one-minute OHLCV in five `f64` fields is 525,600 bars of 40 bytes, about 21 MB per series. A SharedWorker holds it for every tab of the origin and outlives its last tab by about 30 s (`client/hosting.ts:119-122`); a server render's long-lived `Engine` holds it for the life of the process. Fifty symbols of scroll-back is about 1 GB that only `clear` or `clearAll` releases. Step 82 documented the limit (`site/guides/patterns.md:50-69`).

The roadmap sketch (`docs/roadmap.md:132-135`, "Design sketch: eviction and memory limits") proposes "a byte budget per worker, enforced by evicting least-recently-read pages", with one invariant: evicting a page subtracts its extent from coverage, since otherwise its points would read as a confirmed gap that is never refetched. With persistence it keeps the evicted page on disk and reloads it on demand, which needs lazy loading. Architecture §4.10.9 adds a row for eviction (`docs/architecture.md:1777`): it "must neither mark a page dirty nor count as a removal", and "a full rewrite must then keep the records of evicted pages"; §4.10.10 (`:1781`) lists lazy loading and a byte budget for the eager load as later work. This proposal keeps the sketch's core (an engine-wide byte budget, page granularity, least-recently-used order, coverage subtracted on eviction) and decides three things the sketch left open: what is pinned against eviction (§3.4), what eviction does to the disk without lazy loading (§3.6, E11), and how a reader hears of it without refetching what it already shows (§3.7, E16).

Three consequences shape the design. First, a page is already the unit of the segment layout and of the persisted records, so evicting a page drops whole segments and never splits one. Second, because coverage is separate from the data, the safety rule is a coverage subtraction and nothing else: a point that is gone and not covered reads as `uncached`, which the consumer already handles. Third, the persister's dirty marking is by slot range, whatever memory holds, so a page that memory dropped and the disk still holds is replaced or deleted by the first change that touches it (§3.6).

## 3. The design (recommended options taken)

### 3.1 What is counted, and what is not

The budget counts **the bytes the engine's segments have allocated** (E5): for each `DenseSegment`, `capacity × (Σ bytes per field) + ceil(capacity / 8)`. That is exact, cheap to keep up to date (a segment's capacity changes only in `#reserve`), and close to, though not equal to, what the performance suite gates (`test/perf/memory.test.ts`): that suite reads Node's `arrayBuffers` figure, which leaves out typed arrays of 64 bytes or less that V8 keeps on its heap, such as the mask of a segment of 512 slots or fewer, so its bytes per bar can be lower than this count.

| Held by the engine                                                                             | Counted? | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Segment field arrays and masks, at their capacity                                              | yes      | The steady-state memory; a sparse page with many small segments pays the 64-slot minimum each time, and that is real memory                                                                                            |
| Coverage ranges                                                                                | no       | One small object per range, tens per cache in practice (`coverage.ts:6-8`); the orchestrator's comment allows thousands of fragments in a pathological cache (`orchestrator/orchestrator.ts:159-165`), still kilobytes |
| `mergeWarning` lists                                                                           | no       | Built per call and returned (`engine/cache.ts:613-627`); nothing keeps them                                                                                                                                            |
| Read results and snapshots                                                                     | no       | Fresh arrays transferred to the page (`rpc/server.ts:292-294`); the page owns them, as it owns its chart's buffers                                                                                                     |
| Put batches, incoming conversions, the scratch segment of a join, a reallocation's old buffers | no       | Transient within one call (`engine/merge.ts:145-155`, `:270-290`; `segment/dense.ts:308-341`)                                                                                                                          |
| Persistence: a flush's page records, a load's read result                                      | no       | Transient: a flush builds fresh copies for one transaction (`persist/records.ts:87`); a load holds one cache's stored records until `restore` has copied them                                                          |
| Object overhead (segment objects, column records, maps)                                        | no       | Engine-specific and small next to the arrays; reported as excluded                                                                                                                                                     |

The reported figure is therefore a floor of the worker's memory, and a budget is a budget on cached points, not on the process. The docs say so, with the transient peaks: a load holds one cache's records twice for one step, and a get over a year holds its result once more until it is transferred.

### 3.2 Scope and configuration

- **One budget per engine (E2),** shared by every cache of that engine, so it bounds what the sketch wanted bounded: the worker. In a SharedWorker that is every tab of the origin that reaches the same worker URL; in a dedicated worker one tab; in-process one `createClient`; on a server one `Engine`. Two SharedWorkers during a deploy skew have a budget each (finding M5).
- **Configured by the application,** in bytes:

  ```ts
  interface ClientOptions {
    // …§2.1, plus:
    /**
     * Upper bound on the bytes the worker's caches hold (§3.1), shared by
     * every cache and, in a SharedWorker, every tab. Least recently used
     * pages are evicted above it. Default: none, nothing is evicted.
     */
    memoryBudget?: number;
  }
  // ./engine
  new Engine({ memoryBudget?: number });
  ```

  A positive safe integer, or `ConfigError` from `createClient` and the `Engine` constructor.
- **The first value an engine receives holds for its life (E3).** A later client with another value joins without error; its budget is ignored, a development build prints one `console.warn` naming both values, and `memoryStats()` reports the budget in force. This is N20's rule for the dataset fields (`version` and `finalizedUntil` apply at creation; a joining tab cannot rewind the live cache): a stale tab cannot shrink or lift the budget of the tabs already running.
- **Only an engine that holds no cache takes a budget (E20).** The SharedWorker's engine is created before any tab connects, with no options (`entries/worker.ts:42-48`, `:106-107`), so its budget arrives by op. `createClient` sends it after `init-ok` and resolves only after its answer, before the application can open a cache. A budget that reaches an engine already holding a cache is ignored like a second value under E3: a development build warns, and `memoryStats()` shows none in force. No budget therefore ever switches on under state: there is no use order to seed, no byte total to recompute, and no orchestrated get that started without its pin. A tab of an older deploy without the option that opened a cache first keeps the worker unbudgeted until it restarts (finding M5).
- **No default (E4).** Without `memoryBudget` nothing is evicted and 0.1.0's behaviour is unchanged. The memory guide recommends a budget for every application that lets a user browse many series.

### 3.3 Eviction policy

- **Unit: the page (E6).** A cache's page `p` is slots `[p · cap, p · cap + cap − 1]` with `cap = segmentSlotCap`, the unit of the segment layout (N15) and of the persisted records (§4.10.2). Evicting it drops every segment on it, which never splits a segment.
- **Order: least recently used (E7).** Every page with points has a place in one engine-wide order. A **use** is:
  - a read of points: `get` (orchestrated or `cacheOnly`) and a snapshot, for every page the request overlaps that holds points;
  - a write of points: `put`, an orchestrated fetch's put, `hydrate` and `restore`, for every page the call wrote.

  `coverage()`, `invalidate`, `setFinalizedUntil` and the orchestrator's own coverage probes are not uses. A use moves the page to the most recent end; the order is a counter, not a clock (the engine has no timers, `engine/engine.ts:4`, and tests never read one, `docs/testing.md`).
- **Across tabs,** in a SharedWorker every tab's reads and writes are uses of the one order, so a page any tab reads stays. A page shown by a tab that does not read again can be evicted; that tab's copy (its `GetResult`) is unaffected, and the next read of that range fetches it again.
- **When.** Synchronously at the end of every call that wrote points (`put`, a fetch's put, `hydrate`, `restore`) and whenever a pin is released (§3.4), so an engine that pins held above its budget comes back under it when they go, even if no tab writes again; never inside a call between its write and its coverage (`engine/cache.ts:286-308`). It evicts the least recently used unpinned pages until the counted bytes are at or below the budget, or nothing evictable is left.
- **Soft budget (E14).** Pinned pages can hold the engine above its budget, a single read larger than the budget among them. `memoryStats()` reports the excess; the enforcement at their release evicts it.

### 3.4 What is never evicted

A page is pinned, and skipped by eviction, while any of these holds:

1. **A pending orchestrated `get` needs it (E9).** From the get's first round to its read, its request plus one slot on each side (the probe of `orchestrator/orchestrator.ts:524-531`) is pinned. Without the pin, a fetch landing for another tab between this get's fetch and its read could evict what this get just fetched, and the read would answer with `uncached` misses for data it had fetched: truthful, but the consumer would fetch again and could loop. A fetch whose get stopped waiting (auth turned invalid) needs no pin: its put replaces the range it was asked for, whatever memory holds by then.
2. **It holds a point in the volatile region (E8).** The forming tail is small, written constantly and never covered, so eviction would subtract nothing and only drop the bar a live chart shows; with a pushed feed and no fetcher it may be the only copy.
3. **Only with E11 (a) or (c): it is dirty in a running persisted cache (E10).** From the change that marked it until a flush that took it succeeds, or until the cache's persistence stops. Evicting it earlier would let the flush (or its retry, built again from memory, `persist/persister.ts:944-953`) delete a record that those options keep. With the recommended E11 (b) the disk follows memory (§3.6), so no page is pinned for persistence.

Nothing else is pinned: an in-flight fetch's range (its put replaces the range), a cache whose load is pending (it holds no point: any mutating call abandons the load, §4.10.5 rules 2 and 4), and a page shown on a screen (the worker does not know screens).

**A call's own writes are not pinned (E19).** They are the most recent pages, so enforcement takes them last; when one call writes more than the budget holds, the call's own oldest pages go at the end of that call, `restore` included (§3.6). A fetch's put is the case rule 1 covers: its get pins its request until the read, and the enforcement at that release trims it.

### 3.5 The safety rule: coverage goes with the page

Evicting page `p` of a cache, in one synchronous step:

1. drops every segment whose extent lies on `p` (they are contiguous in the store's ascending array);
2. subtracts `p`'s whole slot range from the cache's coverage (E13);
3. lowers the counted bytes, removes `p` from the order, and records `p`'s covered ranges before step 2 as the eviction's report (§3.7).

A `get` over the page afterwards returns no point there and an `uncached` miss for the whole page, so an orchestrated read fetches it again and a `cacheOnly` read says it is missing. No path can read an evicted page as a confirmed gap. Subtracting the whole page, rather than only the segments' extents, also forgets the confirmed gaps on it: they are refetched with the page, which costs a request range that is one page wider than strictly needed and keeps coverage aligned with the persisted records, which are per page.

Eviction changes nothing else: not the version, not the watermark, not another page's coverage, and it is not a mutating call. It reports through a hook of its own, `onEvict` (§3.11), never as a `remove` or `clear` change: persistence marks the page without counting a removal (§3.6), and `onMutate` does not fire (a load is never abandoned by it, and a loading cache holds nothing to evict).

Visible effects that follow from N16 and are documented, not designed around: an invalidated page that is evicted stops showing its stale points while its refetch runs; and a refetch of an evicted page is compared with nothing stored, so `warnOnOverlapDiff` cannot report a restatement there (overlap warnings stay a best-effort check, `site/guides/patterns.md`, "Restatements need a version").

### 3.6 Persistence: the disk follows memory (E11)

Eviction drops the page from memory, and the persister deletes its record at the next flush. Eviction reports the page through its own hook, not `onChange` (§3.11); the persister marks the page dirty as a `write` does, so the flush deletes the page's range and puts what memory holds there, which is nothing. It is not a removal: it does not make the flush strict, does not add to the removal count, and no call waits for it (N58 unchanged). If that deletion never lands (the worker closes first, or the disk refuses it until the deadline), the record stays and a later worker loads it: its data was right when the page was evicted, and every later change to the page marks the page as before (a write, a removal, a clear or a version change), so no stale record outlives a change memory saw.

So after a flush the disk holds `recordsOf(S)`, as §4.10.2 and §4.10.6 property (a) say today, and N46, N57 and N58 are untouched. Two bounds follow. The disk holds at most what memory held at each persisted cache's last flush. The eager load (N46) of one cache reads at most what that cache held then, which, with the same budget, is at most the budget plus what was pinned; the enforcement after `restore` trims it. The load's peak is therefore one cache's records next to the budget.

The cost is the one the roadmap wanted to avoid: evicted history is fetched from the network again, in this session and the next. Two alternatives keep it on disk, and each needs more than this proposal designs (E11):

- (c) Keep the record and reload it only by the next worker's eager load. Safe, since every later change to an evicted page marks it, but unbounded: every page ever evicted stays on disk until the origin's quota, where N47 stops the cache and deletes its records; and the next worker's eager load reads a cache's whole history at once, which can exceed the memory the budget never let the worker reach (a 100 MB budget after a user scrolled through 2 GB of one series). It needs an eager load bounded by the budget (the cache record and the page keys first, then the newest pages that fit, through `read(id, pages)`, `persist/store.ts:37`) and a bound on the disk. It amends §4.10.2 and §4.10.6 property (a): the disk would hold the records of evicted pages besides `recordsOf(S)`. With it, E10 (dirty pages pinned until flushed) and E12 (a full rewrite deletes evicted records) apply.
- (a) Keep the record and load it on demand (lazy loading, §4.10.10), with the same disk bound. It amends N46 and N57, and E10 and E12 apply too.

What E11 (b) amends in approved text: the §4.10.9 eviction row (`docs/architecture.md:1777`), now "Must neither mark a page dirty nor count as a removal: the record stays and is loaded again on demand, which needs lazy loading first; and a full rewrite must then keep the records of evicted pages", becomes "Marks the page dirty as a write does, and is not a removal: the next flush deletes its record, relaxed, and no call waits for it. Keeping evicted pages on disk needs lazy loading and a disk budget (M3, E11)."

### 3.7 Reporting

**A stats call (E15).**

```ts
interface MemoryStats {
  /** The budget in force, in bytes; absent when none. */
  budget?: number;
  /** Bytes the segments of every cache hold, as §3.1 counts them. */
  bytes: number;
  /** Of those, bytes in pages pinned now (§3.4); 0 without a budget. */
  pinnedBytes: number;
  /** Pages evicted since the engine started. */
  evictedPages: number;
  /** One entry per cache, ascending by id. */
  caches: { cacheId: string; bytes: number; pages: number }[];
}

interface TscacheClient {
  // …§2.1, plus:
  /** What the worker's caches hold now (§3.1). */
  memoryStats(): Promise<MemoryStats>;
}
// ./engine
engine.memoryStats(): MemoryStats;
```

It reports the engine behind the client: in a SharedWorker every tab's caches, which is what a tab deciding on a budget needs to see. No per-cache handle method: a cache's share is one row of the same answer.

**No new event.** An application that wants to watch memory polls `memoryStats()` on its own clock, as it does for its own expiry (`site/guides/patterns.md`, "Expiry on your own clock"). An event per eviction would fire in bursts exactly when the worker is busiest.

**Eviction and M7's change event (E16).** M7's draft lists eviction as a change reported in `removed` (`docs/design/m7-change-events.md` §3.2, the eviction row, on step 92's branch). Its read rule then has every view showing the range refetch it (§3.5 there). That is the wrong reaction to an eviction: the data did not change, the view's copy is still right, and a view that refetches what was evicted for lack of room evicts another view's pages, whose views refetch in turn: thrash, across tabs, whenever the views' ranges together exceed the budget. This proposal reports eviction in a list of its own:

```ts
cacheChanged: {
  // …M7's fields, plus:
  /** Coverage dropped to free memory: the data did not change; nothing needs re-reading. */
  evicted: Range[];
}
```

with the read rule extended: an `evicted` range needs no read; a view that reads it later fetches it as any miss. Only the coverage actually withdrawn is listed (as M7's D12 for `removed`). With E16 (a) the M7 row changes from `removed` to `evicted`; if M7 is not built, eviction is visible only through `memoryStats().evictedPages` and the misses of later reads.

**One event per cache per call (M7 §3.2, D3).** An eviction belongs to the report of the call whose enforcement ran it. A put that evicts pages of its own cache sends that cache one `cacheChanged` with its `written` and its `evicted`; pages of another cache evicted by the same call go into that cache's one event for the call, under the same `requestId`. An enforcement at a get's pin release reports in one event per cache, named by that get's `requestId`, as M7 names a fetch.

**A later removal over an evicted range (E21).** With M7's D12 (a), `removed` lists only coverage actually withdrawn. An `invalidate` over a range whose coverage was evicted withdraws nothing and would send no event, so a tab still showing its copy of that range would never learn of the restatement the invalidation announces. With a budget, each cache therefore keeps the coverage it evicted, in a second `CoverageIndex`: a removal (`invalidate`, a watermark moved back or set first) reports what it withdrew plus the part of its range in that index, and drops that part from the index; a write drops what it wrote from the index, since `written` reports it; a clear empties it, since `cleared` covers it.

### 3.8 Interactions

- **`cacheOnly` reads** are uses, so a view that keeps re-reading keeps its pages. Over an evicted range they return `uncached` misses; a reader that only ever reads `cacheOnly` (the reference clients after a `BroadcastChannel` message) must fetch such a miss itself, as for any range never fetched.
- **Orchestrated `get`s** pin their request until their read (§3.4), so a get never answers `uncached` for what it fetched. Joined gets pin their own requests; the in-flight dedup is unchanged.
- **`mergeWarning`** loses what it would have compared on an evicted page (§3.5). Nothing else changes.
- **Version and watermark.** A version-mismatch clear and `clear` drop every page of the cache from the order and the count; eviction never changes either value.
- **`restore`** is followed by enforcement (§3.6), which evicts other caches' least recently used pages first and then the cache's own oldest (E19). It still applies only into a cache that knows nothing; eviction does not abandon a load.
- **`hydrate`** fills only what the cache neither covers nor holds as provisional (N35), so it refills an evicted range from a snapshot as it would fill a fresh cache: correct under N35's own terms, since the snapshot and the cache share a version. Its applied pages are uses.
- **Auth pending** writes nothing and pins nothing beyond the get's own life.
- **`clearAll`** empties the order and the count with the caches.

### 3.9 Cost

- **No budget: zero per operation.** No order is kept, nothing is counted, no pin is registered, no evicted coverage is kept (the orchestrator asks the engine whether a budget exists, a field read). `memoryStats()` computes its figures on demand by walking every cache's segments: `O(caches + segments)` per call, which only the caller pays.
- **With a budget, per call:**
  - a read: one move in the order per page the request overlaps (a JavaScript `Map` delete and set; 1–2 pages for a viewport, 17 for a year of one-minute bars);
  - a write: the capacity change of the segments the store touched (it already visits them), one move per page written, and a comparison with the budget;
  - an orchestrated get: one pin registered and released;
  - an eviction: a binary search for the page's segments, their removal, one `CoverageIndex.subtract` (`coverage.ts:65-82`) and one `covered` for the report when M7 has a subscriber; the page's bytes are freed, so the work is paid for by the memory it releases.
  - with M7 and E21 (a): one `CoverageIndex.add` per eviction and one `covered` per removal against the evicted index;
- **Bench gate.** The memory-per-bar budget is unchanged (no budget set). The contract-test step adds budgets for messages and allocations per read with a budget set, and a stress case that reads a year of each of 50 series through a 100 MB budget and asserts the counted bytes never exceed it outside pins.

### 3.10 Public API, protocol and version

- **Public API, all additive:** `ClientOptions.memoryBudget`, `TscacheClient.memoryStats()`, `EngineOptions.memoryBudget` and `Engine.memoryStats()` on `./engine` (E17), the `MemoryStats` type, and, with M7, the `evicted` field (E16). No `CacheConfig` field, so N20 is untouched.
- **Protocol: additive, `PROTOCOL_VERSION` stays 1 (E18, as N37).** Two ops: `memoryBudget { bytes }`, which the client sends once after `init-ok` when the option is set, resolving `createClient` only after its answer (E20), and whose answer is the budget in force (the client warns in development when it differs), and `memoryStats`. The handshake (§3.1, locked) is untouched. An older worker answers both with `unknown op`: the client then treats the budget as not applied, warns once in development, and `memoryStats()` rejects with `TscacheError`, as `hydrate` does against an older worker (N37). If M7's `hello.features` is adopted, the worker lists `'memoryBudget'` there and the client learns it at connect.
- **Version.** A minor release under 0.x semver; with no default budget nothing changes for an application that does not opt in. Which release carries it (with M7 or after) is the user's call (checkpoint of November 2026, decision 6).

### 3.11 Internal shape (for the implementer; not a public contract)

- `DenseSegment` gains a `bytes` getter (capacity times the row's bytes plus the mask). `SegmentStore` keeps a running total, updated where it already creates, grows, joins, splits or drops segments, and gains `evictPage(page)`, returning the bytes freed.
- `CacheState` gains `evict(page)`, which runs §3.5 and reports through a new internal hook, `onEvict(covered: SlotRange[])`, beside `onChange`; `cacheInternals` exposes nothing new.
- `Engine` keeps the order (one `Map` from `cacheId` and page to nothing, in use order), the total, the pin sets and the enforcement; pins come from the orchestrator (pending gets, through an accessor like `cacheStateOf`), and with E11 (a) or (c) from the persister (dirty pages). `observeCaches` gains `onEvict`, through which the persister marks the evicted page (E11 (b)) and M7's reader report lists it (E16). With a budget, each `CacheState` also keeps the coverage it evicted, as a second `CoverageIndex` (E21). `./engine`'s public events are unchanged.
- The RPC server adds the two ops; the client sends `memoryBudget` after `init-ok`.

## 5. Contract tests for the later step

Engine-logic tests (Codex writes them first, from the approved text), at the level of `SegmentStore`, `CacheState` and `Engine`:

1. **Counting.** For random puts (dense, sparse, growing both ways, joins, K-splits, authority carves), the counted bytes equal the sum over segments of capacity × row bytes + mask bytes, and `memoryStats().bytes` equals it; after `clear`, `clearAll` and a version-mismatch clear the cache counts 0.
2. **No budget, no bookkeeping.** Without a budget, no order is kept, no pin registered and no evicted-coverage index built (a spy on the internal seams), and every get, put and fetch result equals 0.1.0's.
3. **Enforcement.** After any write call with nothing pinned, the counted bytes are at or below the budget; the pages evicted are the least recently used, across caches.
4. **What counts as a use.** A `get`, a `cacheOnly` get, a snapshot, a put, a fetch's put, a `hydrate` and a `restore` move their pages to the recent end; `coverage()`, `invalidate` and `setFinalizedUntil` do not.
5. **The safety rule (property).** For random sequences of puts, gets, invalidations, watermark moves, clears and evictions under a small budget, for every range, a budgeted engine's coverage is a subset of an unbudgeted engine's fed the same calls, and every point inside the budgeted coverage equals the unbudgeted one's: an evicted range never reads as a confirmed gap.
6. **Coverage subtraction.** After evicting a page, a `get` over it returns no point, no coverage and one `uncached` miss for the page's span within the request; neighbouring pages keep their coverage.
7. **Pins.** A page holding a provisional point and the request of a pending get are never evicted; the engine may then exceed the budget, and `pinnedBytes` reports it. When the pin is released, the enforcement at the release brings the bytes under the budget with no further call.
8. **A call larger than the budget (E19).** A put, a `hydrate` and a `restore` that write more than the budget holds complete; at the end of the call other caches' least recently used pages go first, then the call's own oldest pages, and the bytes end at or below the budget.
9. **Eviction is not a removal.** Evicting fires no `onMutate` and reports no `remove` or `clear` through `onChange`; it reports through `onEvict` only.
10. **`restore` then evict.** Loading more pages than the budget holds leaves the bytes at or below it; among the cache's own pages, the oldest go first.
11. **`hydrate` refills** an evicted range of the same version, and its applied pages are recent.
12. **Budget validation, first-wins and switch-on (E3, E20).** A non-positive or unsafe budget throws `ConfigError`; an engine keeps its first budget and reports it; a budget that reaches an engine holding a cache is not applied, and `memoryStats()` reports none in force.

Orchestrator, persister and RPC tests (written with the code, hybrid TDD):

13. **A get's pin.** With a gate holding the second of a get's two fetches, another connection's puts fill the budget; when the gate opens, the get's result holds every point both fetches returned and no `uncached` miss. After the get, its pages are evictable, and the enforcement at its release evicts down to the budget.
14. **Auth release.** A get that stops waiting on `authInvalid` releases its pin; its fetch's later put is applied and then subject to the budget.
15. **Persistence (E11 (b)).** After an eviction and a flush, the evicted page has no record and the flush was relaxed, with no change to the removal count; `durable` answers as before. With the deletion's write failing until the deadline, the record stays, and a reload (§4.10.6 property (b)) loads data equal to what the page held when evicted. Property (a) of §4.10.6 holds unchanged over random sequences with evictions.
16. **Ops.** `memoryBudget` answers the budget in force; a second connection with another value gets the first; `createClient` resolves only after the answer; `memoryStats` reports every cache of the engine; an older worker's `unknown op` leaves `createClient` resolved, warns once in development, and `memoryStats()` rejects with `TscacheError`.
17. **Hostings.** In a dedicated worker and in-process, the budget and stats are per tab's engine.

With M7 (E16 (a), E21 (a)):

18. **One event per cache per call.** A put that evicts pages of its own cache and of another sends one `cacheChanged` per cache: the first with its `written` and its `evicted`, the second with `evicted` only; both carry the put's `requestId`. An enforcement at a get's pin release sends one event per cache with that get's `requestId`. `evicted` equals the coverage withdrawn. Nobody subscribed, nothing is computed.
19. **A later removal over an evicted range.** After page P is evicted, an `invalidate` over it sends `removed` covering P's evicted coverage inside the range; a second `invalidate` sends nothing; a write over part of P, then an `invalidate`, reports only the rest; a watermark moved back over P reports the same way; a `clear` empties the index.

Browser (Playwright, one context, several pages):

20. Two tabs on one SharedWorker share one budget: reads in tab A evict the least recently used pages of a cache tab B read earlier; tab B's next `get` over them fetches again (the backend's fetch count rises by the evicted pages), and `memoryStats()` agrees in both tabs.
21. A persisted cache under a small budget: after a flush and a reload, the evicted pages are not loaded and a read fetches them; the pages still resident load from disk without a fetch.

Bench: per-read and per-write cost budgets with a budget set; the existing memory-per-bar and messages-per-read budgets unchanged without one.
