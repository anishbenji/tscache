It answers finding M3 of the review of 2026-11-10 (`docs/reviews/2026-11-review.md`, M3 and §3c) under user decision 46 A: after 0.1.0, three design steps in the order M7, M3, M5, each a spec first, then the user's decision rows, then contract tests. ## 1. The gap

Nothing bounds the memory a cache holds, and nothing reports it. No eviction, budget or size call exists in `packages/tscache/src`; the public surface is `CacheHandle` (`get`, `put`, `hydrate`, `invalidate`, `clear`, `setFinalizedUntil`, `on`) and the client's `cache`, `clearAll`, `updateAuth`, `on`, `off`, `dispose` (`client/cache.ts:41-107`, `client/client.ts:43-71`). One year of one-minute OHLCV in five `f64` fields is 525,600 bars of 40 bytes, about 21 MB per series. A SharedWorker holds it for every tab of the origin and outlives its last tab by about 30 s (`client/hosting.ts:119-122`); a server render's long-lived `Engine` holds it for the life of the process. Fifty symbols of scroll-back is about 1 GB that only `clear` or `clearAll` releases. Step 82 documented the limit (`site/guides/patterns.md:50-69`).

The roadmap sketch (`docs/roadmap.md:123-128`, "Design sketch: eviction and memory limits") proposes "a byte budget per worker, enforced by evicting least-recently-read pages", with one invariant: evicting a page subtracts its extent from coverage, since otherwise its points would read as a confirmed gap that is never refetched. With persistence it keeps the evicted page on disk and reloads it on demand, which needs lazy loading. Architecture §4.10.9 adds a row for eviction (`docs/architecture.md:1777`): it "must neither mark a page dirty nor count as a removal", and "a full rewrite must then keep the records of evicted pages"; §4.10.10 (`:1781`) lists lazy loading and a byte budget for the eager load as later work. This proposal keeps the sketch's core (an engine-wide byte budget, page granularity, least-recently-used order, coverage subtracted on eviction) and decides three things the sketch left open: what is pinned against eviction (§3.4), what eviction does to the disk without lazy loading (§3.6, E11), and how a reader hears of it without refetching what it already shows (§3.7, E16).

Three consequences shape the design. First, a page is already the unit of the segment layout and of the persisted records, so evicting a page drops whole segments and never splits one. Second, because coverage is separate from the data, the safety rule is a coverage subtraction and nothing else: a point that is gone and not covered reads as `uncached`, which the consumer already handles. Third, the persister's dirty marking is by slot range, whatever memory holds, so a page that memory dropped and the disk still holds is replaced or deleted by the first change that touches it (§3.6).

## 3. The design (recommended options taken)

### 3.1 What is counted, and what is not

The budget counts **the bytes the engine's segments have allocated** (E5): for each `DenseSegment`, `capacity × (Σ bytes per field) + ceil(capacity / 8)`. That is exact, cheap to keep up to date (a segment's capacity changes only in `#reserve`), and the quantity the performance suite already gates (ArrayBuffer bytes per bar).

| Held by the engine                                                                             | Counted? | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Segment field arrays and masks, at their capacity                                              | yes      | The steady-state memory; a sparse page with many small segments pays the 64-slot minimum each time, and that is real memory                                                                                            |
| Coverage ranges                                                                                | no       | One small object per range, tens per cache in practice (`coverage.ts:6-8`); the orchestrator's comment allows thousands of fragments in a pathological cache (`orchestrator/orchestrator.ts:159-165`), still kilobytes |
| `mergeWarning` lists                                                                           | no       | Built per call and returned (`engine/cache.ts:613-627`); nothing keeps them                                                                                                                                            |
| Read results and snapshots                                                                     | no       | Fresh arrays transferred to the page (`rpc/server.ts:292-294`); the page owns them, as it owns its chart's buffers                                                                                                     |
| Put batches, incoming conversions, the scratch segment of a join, a reallocation's old buffers | no       | Transient within one call (`engine/merge.ts:145-155`, `:270-290`; `segment/dense.ts:308-341`)                                                                                                                          |
| Persistence: a flush's page records, a load's read result                                      | no       | Transient: a flush builds fresh copies for one transaction (`persist/records.ts:87`); a load holds one cache's stored records until `restore` has copied them                                                          |
| Object overhead (segment objects, column records, maps)                                        | no       | Engine-specific and small next to the arrays; reported as excluded                                                                                                                                                     |

The reported figure is therefore a floor of the worker's memory, and a budget is a budget on cached points, not on the process. The docs say so, with the transient peaks: a load holds one cache's records twice for one step, and a get over a year holds its result once more until it is transferred.

### 3.2 Scope and configuration

- **One budget per engine (E2),** shared by every cache of that engine, so it bounds what the sketch wanted bounded: the worker. In a SharedWorker that is every tab of the origin that reaches the same worker URL; in a dedicated worker one tab; in-process one `createClient`; on a server one `Engine`. Two SharedWorkers during a deploy skew have a budget each (finding M5).
- **Configured by the application,** in bytes:

  ```ts
  interface ClientOptions {
    // …§2.1, plus:
    /**
     * Upper bound on the bytes the worker's caches hold (§3.1), shared by
     * every cache and, in a SharedWorker, every tab. Least recently used
     * pages are evicted above it. Default: none, nothing is evicted.
     */
    memoryBudget?: number;
  }
  // ./engine
  new Engine({ memoryBudget?: number });
  ```

  A positive safe integer, or `ConfigError` from `createClient` and the `Engine` constructor.
- **The first value an engine receives holds for its life (E3).** A later client with another value joins without error; its budget is ignored, a development build prints one `console.warn` naming both values, and `memoryStats()` reports the budget in force. This is N20's rule for the dataset fields (`version` and `finalizedUntil` apply at creation; a joining tab cannot rewind the live cache): a stale tab cannot shrink or lift the budget of the tabs already running.
- **No default (E4).** Without `memoryBudget` nothing is evicted and 0.1.0's behaviour is unchanged. The memory guide recommends a budget for every application that lets a user browse many series.

### 3.3 Eviction policy

- **Unit: the page (E6).** A cache's page `p` is slots `[p · cap, p · cap + cap − 1]` with `cap = segmentSlotCap`, the unit of the segment layout (N15) and of the persisted records (§4.10.2). Evicting it drops every segment on it, which never splits a segment.
- **Order: least recently used (E7).** Every page with points has a place in one engine-wide order. A **use** is:
  - a read of points: `get` (orchestrated or `cacheOnly`) and a snapshot, for every page the request overlaps that holds points;
  - a write of points: `put`, an orchestrated fetch's put, `hydrate` and `restore`, for every page the call wrote.

  `coverage()`, `invalidate`, `setFinalizedUntil` and the orchestrator's own coverage probes are not uses. A use moves the page to the most recent end; the order is a counter, not a clock (the engine has no timers, `engine/engine.ts:4`, and tests never read one, `docs/testing.md`).
- **Across tabs,** in a SharedWorker every tab's reads and writes are uses of the one order, so a page any tab reads stays. A page shown by a tab that does not read again can be evicted; that tab's copy (its `GetResult`) is unaffected, and the next read of that range fetches it again.
- **When.** Synchronously at the end of every call that wrote points (`put`, a fetch's put, `hydrate`, `restore`) and when a pin is released (§3.4), never inside a call between its write and its coverage (`engine/cache.ts:286-308`). It evicts the least recently used unpinned pages until the counted bytes are at or below the budget, or nothing evictable is left.
- **Soft budget (E14).** Pinned pages can hold the engine above its budget, a single read larger than the budget among them. `memoryStats()` reports the excess; the next enforcement after the pins go evicts it.

### 3.4 What is never evicted

A page is pinned, and skipped by eviction, while any of these holds:

1. **A pending orchestrated `get` needs it (E9).** From the get's first round to its read, its request plus one slot on each side (the probe of `orchestrator/orchestrator.ts:524-531`) is pinned. Without the pin, a fetch landing for another tab between this get's fetch and its read could evict what this get just fetched, and the read would answer with `uncached` misses for data it had fetched: truthful, but the consumer would fetch again and could loop. A fetch whose get stopped waiting (auth turned invalid) needs no pin: its put replaces the range it was asked for, whatever memory holds by then.
2. **It holds a point in the volatile region (E8).** The forming tail is small, written constantly and never covered, so eviction would subtract nothing and only drop the bar a live chart shows; with a pushed feed and no fetcher it may be the only copy.
3. **It is dirty in a running persisted cache (E10).** From the change that marked it until a flush that took it succeeds, or until the cache's persistence stops. Evicting it earlier would let the flush (or its retry, built again from memory, `persist/persister.ts:944-953`) delete the page's record instead of writing its new content.
4. **The call that triggers the enforcement wrote it.** A put larger than the budget keeps what it just wrote until the next enforcement.

Nothing else is pinned: an in-flight fetch's range (its put replaces the range), a cache whose load is pending (it holds no point: any mutating call abandons the load, §4.10.5 rules 2 and 4), and a page shown on a screen (the worker does not know screens).

### 3.5 The safety rule: coverage goes with the page

Evicting page `p` of a cache, in one synchronous step:

1. drops every segment whose extent lies on `p` (they are contiguous in the store's ascending array);
2. subtracts `p`'s whole slot range from the cache's coverage (E13);
3. lowers the counted bytes, removes `p` from the order, and records `p`'s covered ranges before step 2 as the eviction's report (§3.7).

A `get` over the page afterwards returns no point there and an `uncached` miss for the whole page, so an orchestrated read fetches it again and a `cacheOnly` read says it is missing. No path can read an evicted page as a confirmed gap. Subtracting the whole page, rather than only the segments' extents, also forgets the confirmed gaps on it: they are refetched with the page, which costs a request range that is one page wider than strictly needed and keeps coverage aligned with the persisted records, which are per page.

Eviction changes nothing else: not the version, not the watermark, not another page's coverage, and it is not a mutating call. It does not report through `onChange` (persistence must not mark the page or count a removal, §4.10.9) and does not fire `onMutate` (a load is never abandoned by it, and a loading cache holds nothing to evict).

Visible effects that follow from N16 and are documented, not designed around: an invalidated page that is evicted stops showing its stale points while its refetch runs; and a refetch of an evicted page is compared with nothing stored, so `warnOnOverlapDiff` cannot report a restatement there (overlap warnings stay a best-effort check, `site/guides/patterns.md`, "Restatements need a version").

### 3.6 Persistence: memory only, the record stays (E11, E12)

Eviction drops the page from memory and leaves its record on disk. Nothing is reloaded within the session: a later read of the range fetches it from the network, and the next worker's eager load (N46) brings it back. Lazy loading on first read, which would serve the read from disk instead, stays the later step §4.10.10 describes.

Why it is safe without lazy loading. The disk then holds `recordsOf(S)` plus the records of pages evicted since they were last written. Every change that could make such a record wrong marks its page or the whole cache, whatever memory holds:

| After a page was evicted                                               | What the persister does                                                                         | Its record                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A put, a fetch or a `hydrate` writes part of it                        | marks the page dirty (`write`); the flush deletes the page range and puts what memory now holds | replaced by memory's content, which may cover less than the record did: cached data lost, nothing wrong kept |
| `invalidate` over it                                                   | `remove` marks it dirty; the flush deletes it, strict                                           | deleted                                                                                                      |
| `setFinalizedUntil` back, or a first watermark                         | `remove [volatileFrom, MAX_SAFE_INTEGER]` marks it                                              | deleted                                                                                                      |
| `clear`, `clearAll`, a version-mismatch clear, a first version adopted | `rewrite`: every page deleted (E12)                                                             | deleted                                                                                                      |
| The watermark moves forward                                            | only the cache record is rewritten; a page record carries no watermark (§4.10.2)                | kept, correctly                                                                                              |
| Nothing touches it                                                     | nothing                                                                                         | kept; loaded by the next worker, and evicted again if the budget needs it                                    |

A stored record is therefore never older than the last change to its page that memory saw. The next load still applies records only into a cache that knows nothing (N57, unchanged), and validates each against the version and structure (N56, unchanged).

The eager load is unchanged too: it applies every stored page, then the enforcement that follows `restore` evicts down to the budget. `restore` uses the pages in ascending order, so a cache's oldest pages are the least recent of what it loaded, and pages other caches have not used are older still. The load's peak is one cache's stored records, applied and then trimmed; a byte budget for the eager load itself (read only the newest pages that fit) stays in §4.10.10 with lazy loading.

What this amends in approved text, for the user to approve with E11 and E12:

- §4.10.2, "Canonical records" (`docs/architecture.md:1371`): "what the disk holds after it" becomes "what the disk holds after it, apart from the records of pages evicted since they were last written (M3)".
- §4.10.6, property (a) (`:1685`): the store deep-equals `recordsOf(S)` when nothing was evicted; with evictions, it equals `recordsOf(S)` plus, for each evicted page not marked since, the record written before its eviction.
- §4.10.9, the eviction row (`:1777`): the first clause stands (no dirty mark, no removal); "loaded again on demand" becomes "loaded again by the next worker's load, or on demand once lazy loading exists"; and the last clause, "a full rewrite must then keep the records of evicted pages", is replaced by "a full rewrite deletes them" (E12).

### 3.7 Reporting

**A stats call (E15).**

```ts
interface MemoryStats {
  /** The budget in force, in bytes; absent when none. */
  budget?: number;
  /** Bytes the segments of every cache hold, as §3.1 counts them. */
  bytes: number;
  /** Of those, bytes in pages pinned now (§3.4); 0 without a budget. */
  pinnedBytes: number;
  /** Pages evicted since the engine started. */
  evictedPages: number;
  /** One entry per cache, ascending by id. */
  caches: { cacheId: string; bytes: number; pages: number }[];
}

interface TscacheClient {
  // …§2.1, plus:
  /** What the worker's caches hold now (§3.1). */
  memoryStats(): Promise<MemoryStats>;
}
// ./engine
engine.memoryStats(): MemoryStats;
```

It reports the engine behind the client: in a SharedWorker every tab's caches, which is what a tab deciding on a budget needs to see. No per-cache handle method: a cache's share is one row of the same answer.

**No new event.** An application that wants to watch memory polls `memoryStats()` on its own clock, as it does for its own expiry (`site/guides/patterns.md`, "Expiry on your own clock"). An event per eviction would fire in bursts exactly when the worker is busiest.

**Eviction and M7's change event (E16).** M7's draft lists eviction as a change reported in `removed` (`docs/design/m7-change-events.md` §3.2, last row, on step 92's branch). Its read rule then has every view showing the range refetch it (§3.5 there). That is the wrong reaction to an eviction: the data did not change, the view's copy is still right, and a view that refetches what was evicted for lack of room evicts another view's pages, whose views refetch in turn: thrash, across tabs, whenever the views' ranges together exceed the budget. This proposal reports eviction in a list of its own:

```ts
cacheChanged: {
  // …M7's fields, plus:
  /** Coverage dropped to free memory: the data did not change; nothing needs re-reading. */
  evicted: Range[];
}
```

with the read rule extended: an `evicted` range needs no read; a view that reads it later fetches it as any miss. Only the coverage actually withdrawn is listed (as M7's D12 for `removed`). With E16 (a) the M7 row changes from `removed` to `evicted`; if M7 is not built, eviction is visible only through `memoryStats().evictedPages` and the misses of later reads.

### 3.8 Interactions

- **`cacheOnly` reads** are uses, so a view that keeps re-reading keeps its pages. Over an evicted range they return `uncached` misses; a reader that only ever reads `cacheOnly` (the reference clients after a `BroadcastChannel` message) must fetch such a miss itself, as for any range never fetched.
- **Orchestrated `get`s** pin their request until their read (§3.4), so a get never answers `uncached` for what it fetched. Joined gets pin their own requests; the in-flight dedup is unchanged.
- **`mergeWarning`** loses what it would have compared on an evicted page (§3.5). Nothing else changes.
- **Version and watermark.** A version-mismatch clear and `clear` drop every page of the cache from the order and the count; eviction never changes either value.
- **`restore`** is followed by enforcement (§3.6). It still applies only into a cache that knows nothing; eviction does not abandon a load.
- **`hydrate`** fills only what the cache neither covers nor holds as provisional (N35), so it refills an evicted range from a snapshot as it would fill a fresh cache: correct under N35's own terms, since the snapshot and the cache share a version. Its applied pages are uses.
- **Auth pending** writes nothing and pins nothing beyond the get's own life.
- **`clearAll`** empties the order and the count with the caches.

### 3.9 Cost

- **No budget: zero per operation.** No order is kept, nothing is counted, no pin is registered (the orchestrator asks the engine whether a budget exists, a field read). `memoryStats()` computes its figures on demand by walking every cache's segments: `O(caches + segments)` per call, which only the caller pays.
- **With a budget, per call:**
  - a read: one move in the order per page the request overlaps (a JavaScript `Map` delete and set; 1–2 pages for a viewport, 17 for a year of one-minute bars);
  - a write: the capacity change of the segments the store touched (it already visits them), one move per page written, and a comparison with the budget;
  - an orchestrated get: one pin registered and released;
  - an eviction: a binary search for the page's segments, their removal, one `CoverageIndex.subtract` (`coverage.ts:65-82`) and one `covered` for the report when M7 has a subscriber; the page's bytes are freed, so the work is paid for by the memory it releases.
- **Bench gate.** The memory-per-bar budget is unchanged (no budget set). The contract-test step adds budgets for messages and allocations per read with a budget set, and a stress case that reads a year of each of 50 series through a 100 MB budget and asserts the counted bytes never exceed it outside pins.

### 3.10 Public API, protocol and version

- **Public API, all additive:** `ClientOptions.memoryBudget`, `TscacheClient.memoryStats()`, `EngineOptions.memoryBudget` and `Engine.memoryStats()` on `./engine` (E17), the `MemoryStats` type, and, with M7, the `evicted` field (E16). No `CacheConfig` field, so N20 is untouched.
- **Protocol: additive, `PROTOCOL_VERSION` stays 1 (E18, as N37).** Two ops: `memoryBudget { bytes }`, which the client sends once after `init-ok` when the option is set and whose answer is the budget in force (the client warns in development when it differs), and `memoryStats`. The handshake (§3.1, locked) is untouched. An older worker answers both with `unknown op`: the client then treats the budget as not applied, warns once in development, and `memoryStats()` rejects with `TscacheError`, as `hydrate` does against an older worker (N37). If M7's `hello.features` is adopted, the worker lists `'memoryBudget'` there and the client learns it at connect.
- **Version.** A minor release under 0.x semver; with no default budget nothing changes for an application that does not opt in. Which release carries it (with M7 or after) is the user's call (checkpoint of November 2026, decision 6).

### 3.11 Internal shape (for the implementer; not a public contract)

- `DenseSegment` gains a `bytes` getter (capacity times the row's bytes plus the mask). `SegmentStore` keeps a running total, updated where it already creates, grows, joins, splits or drops segments, and gains `evictPage(page)`, returning the bytes freed.
- `CacheState` gains `evict(page)`, which runs §3.5 and reports through a new internal hook, `onEvict(covered: SlotRange[])`, beside `onChange`; `cacheInternals` exposes nothing new to persistence.
- `Engine` keeps the order (one `Map` from `cacheId` and page to nothing, in use order), the total, the pin sets and the enforcement; pins come from two internal providers: the orchestrator (pending gets, through an accessor like `cacheStateOf`) and the persister (dirty pages). `observeCaches` gains `onEvict` for M7's reader report. `./engine`'s public events are unchanged.
- The RPC server adds the two ops; the client sends `memoryBudget` after `init-ok`.

## 5. Contract tests for the later step

Engine-logic tests (Codex writes them first, from the approved text), at the level of `SegmentStore`, `CacheState` and `Engine`:

1. **Counting.** For random puts (dense, sparse, growing both ways, joins, K-splits, authority carves), the counted bytes equal the sum over segments of capacity × row bytes + mask bytes, and `memoryStats().bytes` equals it; after `clear`, `clearAll` and a version-mismatch clear the cache counts 0.
2. **No budget, no bookkeeping.** Without a budget, no order is kept and no pin registered (a spy on the internal seams), and every get, put and fetch result equals 0.1.0's.
3. **Enforcement.** After any write call with nothing pinned, the counted bytes are at or below the budget; the pages evicted are the least recently used, across caches.
4. **What counts as a use.** A `get`, a `cacheOnly` get, a snapshot, a put, a fetch's put, a `hydrate` and a `restore` move their pages to the recent end; `coverage()`, `invalidate` and `setFinalizedUntil` do not.
5. **The safety rule (property).** For random sequences of puts, gets, invalidations, watermark moves, clears and evictions under a small budget, for every range, a budgeted engine's coverage is a subset of an unbudgeted engine's fed the same calls, and every point inside the budgeted coverage equals the unbudgeted one's: an evicted range never reads as a confirmed gap.
6. **Coverage subtraction.** After evicting a page, a `get` over it returns no point, no coverage and one `uncached` miss for the page's span within the request; neighbouring pages keep their coverage.
7. **Pins.** A page holding a provisional point, a dirty page of a running persisted cache (until its flush succeeds; released at a stop), and the pages of the triggering call are never evicted; the engine may then exceed the budget, and `pinnedBytes` reports it.
8. **Soft budget.** A single put or read larger than the budget completes and answers in full; the next enforcement after its pins go brings the bytes under the budget.
9. **Eviction is not a change to persistence.** Evicting reports no `onChange` and fires no `onMutate`; a persister observing the engine marks nothing and counts no removal.
10. **`restore` then evict.** Loading more pages than the budget holds keeps the most recent ones; the oldest stored pages of the cache go first.
11. **`hydrate` refills** an evicted range of the same version, and its applied pages are recent.
12. **Budget validation and first-wins.** A non-positive or unsafe budget throws `ConfigError`; an engine keeps its first budget and reports it.

Orchestrator, persister and RPC tests (written with the code, hybrid TDD):

13. **A get's pin.** With a gate holding the second of a get's two fetches, another connection's puts fill the budget; when the gate opens, the get's result holds every point both fetches returned and no `uncached` miss. After the get, its pages are evictable.
14. **Auth release.** A get that stops waiting on `authInvalid` releases its pin; its fetch's later put is applied and then subject to the budget.
15. **Persistence (E11 (c)).** An evicted clean page's record stays after a flush; a reload (§4.10.6 property (b)) loads it. A write, an `invalidate`, a watermark moved back over an evicted page replaces or deletes its record; a `clear`, a version change and an abandoned load delete it (E12). Property: over random sequences with evictions, every stored page record equals either `recordsOf(S)` for its page or the record written before its eviction, with no change to that page since.
16. **Dirty pin and retries.** With the store failing the first write, a dirty page stays resident through the retry, and the retry writes its content.
17. **Ops.** `memoryBudget` answers the budget in force; a second connection with another value gets the first; `memoryStats` reports every cache of the engine; an older worker's `unknown op` leaves `createClient` resolved, warns once in development, and `memoryStats()` rejects with `TscacheError`.
18. **Hostings.** In a dedicated worker and in-process, the budget and stats are per tab's engine.
19. With M7 (E16 (a)): an eviction sends one `cacheChanged` with `evicted` equal to the coverage withdrawn and empty `written` and `removed`; nobody subscribed, nothing is computed.

Browser (Playwright, one context, several pages):

20. Two tabs on one SharedWorker share one budget: reads in tab A evict the least recently used pages of a cache tab B read earlier; tab B's next `get` over them fetches again (the backend's fetch count rises by the evicted pages), and `memoryStats()` agrees in both tabs.
21. A persisted cache under a small budget: after a reload, the evicted pages load from disk (no fetch for them).

Bench: per-read and per-write cost budgets with a budget set; the existing memory-per-bar and messages-per-read budgets unchanged without one.
