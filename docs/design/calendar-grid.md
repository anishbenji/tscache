## 1. The gap

The grid is `alignmentOffset + k × interval` in fixed milliseconds (§4.1, N9). Two common shapes of OHLC data do not fit it:

- **Daily bars stamped at a session open in a daylight-saving zone.** A session that opens at 09:30 New York time opens at 14:30 UTC in winter and 13:30 UTC in summer. No `alignmentOffset` fits both, so a batch stamped at the open is rejected whole as `'misaligned'` for half the year.
- **Weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly bars.** Weekly bars fit a fixed 604,800,000 ms step only in UTC; a week that starts at local midnight moves by an hour twice a year. Months, quarters and years have no fixed length at all.

Step 71 published recipes for both (§2.3): stamp a daily bar at 00:00 UTC of its trading date, stamp weekly bars on a fixed UTC weekday, and map months to a synthetic ordinal grid of interval 1 in the fetcher. They work, at a price the application pays: a daily cache holds dates rather than instants, and a monthly cache holds no times at all, so every range, watermark, result and event of it is an ordinal the fetcher and the chart must convert. The checkpoint of November 2026 (`~/code/tscache-reports/proposals/checkpoint-2026-11.md`, part 1, rank 8) calls this a real user-facing gap for an OHLC chart cache, and it touches a locked area.

### 2.4 What follows

Everything inside the engine except `grid.ts` runs on integer slot indices. Coverage, segments, pages, the gap rule, the slot cap, the in-flight subtraction and the persisted page keys never see a millisecond. A grid whose slots have different lengths is therefore, inside the engine, a change to the two primitives of `grid.ts` and nothing else, as the review's §3b noted. The cost lies at the edges where the grid is _named_ rather than _used_: the config and its N20 comparison, the payload and snapshot envelope and their JSON form, the cache record, the fetch request, the domain checks that assume every safe-integer slot has a timestamp, and the code outside the package that does grid arithmetic in milliseconds.

## 4. The options

The brief names four options. For each: the public API, what it costs every engine path that assumes fixed slots today, its bundle size against the budgets, and how it behaves with snapshots and persistence when records written under one grid are read under another.

### 4.1 (a) Recipes only, unchanged

- **API:** none. The four recipes of §2.3 stay; the docs keep the limit.
- **Engine cost:** none.
- **Bundle:** none.
- **Snapshots and persistence:** unchanged; a recipe cache is an ordinary fixed-grid cache, and its records are what the fetcher stamped. A change of recipe (for example from dates to ordinals) is a change of meaning the engine cannot see: the grid may be the same (`interval: 1` holds day ordinals and month ordinals alike), so records written under one mapping load silently under the other unless the application changes the cache's id or `version`.
- **Who pays:** every application with daily bars in a DST zone or with monthly bars, in conversions at the fetcher and at the chart, and the risk above.

### 4.2 (b) A pluggable grid in the config

The application supplies the two primitives: `slotOf(t)` (the slot at or before `t`) and `startOf(k)` (the timestamp of slot `k`), and the engine derives `isAligned`, the snaps and the watermark slot from them. Next and previous slots are `k ± 1` and need no function of their own.

- **API:** a config cannot carry a function across the port (`client/client.ts:208-215`, a structured clone), so the grid must be code the worker loads. The only application code a worker loads is the fetcher module (`rpc/server.ts:260-269`, N44); the fetcher module would export named grids, and the config would name one: `interval: { grid: "xnys-daily" }`. An application without a fetcher module (one that only hydrates and puts) needs a second way to load code into the worker, and the page needs the same functions for its own range arithmetic (§5.8).
- **Engine cost:** every path of §2.1 calls the grid through an interface instead of doing arithmetic, one call per point on the put and read loops (`engine/batch.ts:133`, `:297`; `engine/read.ts:83`); the domain checks (`grid.ts:107-116`, `:153-157`, `orchestrator/orchestrator.ts:75-77`, `segment/payload.ts:54`) ask the grid for its bounds. Correctness becomes the application's: a function that is not strictly increasing, not deterministic, or that differs between the page, the worker and the server corrupts coverage silently (a slot that maps to the wrong time is a confirmed gap or a misplaced bar). The engine can sample-check monotonicity at `cache()`, not prove it.
- **Bundle:** small in the package: the dispatch and the loading path, an estimated few hundred bytes on the worker and engine entries. The calendar code is the application's.
- **Snapshots and persistence:** the grid's identity is its name, which the application must change whenever the function's results change; records and snapshots written under an old function with the same name load as if nothing changed. N20 compares the name.

### 4.3 (c) Built-in calendar grids keyed by IANA zone and session

A small closed set of calendar units, described by data: `{ unit: "day" | "week" | "month" | "quarter" | "year", zone?, at?, weekStart? }`. The descriptor is structured-cloneable and comparable by value, so it crosses the port, serialises into snapshots and records, and takes part in N20 like any other field.

- **API:** the descriptor in `CacheConfig.interval` (G4), mirrored in the fetch request (G10) and the snapshot envelope (G9); a helper entry for the page (G11). §5 has the details.
- **Engine cost:** the same paths as (b), but with code the library owns and tests. In UTC the conversions are integer calendar arithmetic (days from a civil date and back), a few operations per call. In another zone each slot's start needs the zone's UTC offset at that local time, which the runtime's `Intl.DateTimeFormat` gives through `formatToParts`; that is far slower than a multiplication, so the engine keeps computed slot starts in tables shared by every cache of one descriptor (G19, §5.7). The slot at or before an arbitrary `t` needs only slot starts: the UTC date of `t` gives a candidate slot within two slots of the answer (§5.2).
- **Zone data:** the runtime's own (`Intl`), so nothing is bundled. Bundling a zone database was considered and rejected (G3): even a packed subset of the IANA database is many kilobytes, comparable to or larger than the worker entry's whole 13,728 B budget, and it ages with every IANA release. `Temporal` would do the zone arithmetic natively, but the package targets ES2023 (`tsconfig.json:4`) and cannot assume it in every supported browser; a polyfill also costs more than the worker's budget.
- **`Intl` limits:** a zone name the runtime does not know throws `RangeError` (reported as `ConfigError`); `Intl` converts an instant to local fields but not local fields to an instant, so the inverse is a guess-and-correct step of two or three calls; a local time that does not exist (the spring-forward gap) or exists twice (the fall-back overlap) needs a rule (G6), and so does a whole local date a zone skipped when it moved across the date line (G18); the zone rules are the runtime's ICU data, which differ between browser versions, and between a server's Node and a browser (G8); a runtime built without time-zone data cannot resolve any zone but UTC.
- **Bundle:** estimated 1–2 KB brotli on the `worker` and `engine` entries (calendar arithmetic, the `Intl` offset lookup, the shared tables of slot starts (G19) and the descriptor's validation), measured by `scripts/size.ts` in the build step. That exceeds the 3 % headroom of both budgets, so the build step raises them in a reviewed edit with the reason. The `index` entry grows by the client's feature check (G14) and by `decodeSnapshot`'s format 2 branch, since the root entry exports the JSON form (`entries/index.ts:26`); the page helpers live in their own entry (G11).
- **Snapshots and persistence:** the descriptor names the grid in the envelope, payload and cache record, and in their JSON form for server rendering (N38), compared by value; a cache of another grid refuses a snapshot and does not apply records, as today. Within one descriptor, a change of the runtime's zone rules can move a slot's start; §5.6 and G8 say what happens then.

### 4.4 (d) An ordinal grid the user maps

The monthly recipe made a library feature: the engine stores ordinals on a grid of interval 1, and a mapping layer in the page converts between milliseconds and ordinals at every call: ranges, timestamps and watermarks going in; timestamps, coverage, misses and event ranges coming out. The mapping is two functions given to `client.cache()` and kept in the page, never sent to the worker.

- **API:** an option of `client.cache()` that the page-side handle applies (`ordinal: { toOrdinal(t), toTime(n) }`); the worker sees an ordinary `interval: 1` cache.
- **Engine cost:** none; N9 and every engine path are untouched. The cost moves to the page: an extra pass over every result's timestamps on the main thread, a conversion of every event range, and a fetcher that still receives ordinals, because the fetcher runs in the worker, where the mapping is not (the fetcher module must carry the same functions, as in today's recipe).
- **Bundle:** small, on the `index` entry (the mapping layer), which every application loads; an estimated few hundred bytes.
- **Snapshots and persistence:** they hold ordinals. The mapping is invisible to the engine, so two mappings over `interval: 1` look identical to N20, to `hydrate` and to a load: a changed mapping silently re-labels every stored record unless the application changes the id or `version`. A server-rendered snapshot is correct only when the server maps as the page does.

## 5. The design (recommended options taken)

### 5.1 The descriptor

```ts
/** A calendar grid: one slot per calendar unit in a time zone (G1 c). */
interface CalendarInterval {
  /** G2 (a). */
  unit: "day" | "week" | "month" | "quarter" | "year";
  /** An IANA time zone, compared as written (G7). Default "UTC". */
  zone?: string;
  /** Local wall-clock time each slot starts at, "HH:MM" (24-hour). Default "00:00". */
  at?: string;
  /** For unit "week" only: the first day, 1 = Monday … 7 = Sunday (ISO 8601). Default 1. */
  weekStart?: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7;
}

interface CacheConfig {
  /** A fixed interval in ms, or a calendar interval (G4 a). Required, no default (decision 2). */
  interval: number | CalendarInterval;
  /** Fixed intervals only; must be absent or 0 with a calendar interval (G4). */
  alignmentOffset?: number;
  // … unchanged
}
```

Examples:

```ts
// Daily bars of a New York session, stamped at the open, all year.
{ id: "candles/ACME/1d", interval: { unit: "day", zone: "America/New_York", at: "09:30" }, fields }
// Weekly bars starting Monday at local midnight in London.
{ id: "candles/ACME/1w", interval: { unit: "week", zone: "Europe/London" }, fields }
// Monthly bars at 00:00 UTC of the first day.
{ id: "candles/BTC/1mo", interval: { unit: "month" }, fields }
```

The resolved config holds the descriptor with its defaults applied, frozen, `weekStart` present only for weeks. A fixed config resolves exactly as today.

### 5.2 Slots and the two primitives

- **Slot numbering.** Slot 0 is the unit that contains the local date 1970-01-01 in the grid's zone: for days, the local day number since that date; for weeks, the week containing it (a Thursday), so with `weekStart: 1` slot 0 starts on Monday 1969-12-29; for months, `(year − 1970) × 12 + (month − 1)`; for quarters, `(year − 1970) × 4 + ⌊(month − 1) / 3⌋`; for years, `year − 1970`. Slots before 1970 are negative, as today. The numbering depends only on the descriptor, never on the data or on the zone's rules, so page keys (N15) are stable across rule changes.
- **`startOf(k)`**, the generalised `msOf`: the instant at which the local date that begins unit `k` reads `at` on the wall clock of the zone. In UTC it is calendar arithmetic. Elsewhere it resolves the local time through the zone's offset, with the rule of G6 for a local time that does not exist or exists twice. `startOf` must be strictly increasing in `k`, since coverage and `toMs` rely on distinct slots having distinct starts. Daylight-saving shifts never break that, but a zone that moved across the date line skipped a whole local date: Pacific/Apia has no 2011-12-30 (its clock went from 2011-12-29 23:59 at UTC−10 to 2011-12-31 00:00 at UTC+14, at 2011-12-30 10:00 UTC), and Pacific/Kwajalein has no 1993-08-21 (from UTC−12 to UTC+12 at 1993-08-21 12:00 UTC); both are in the runtime's zone data. G6 (a) alone would give the skipped date's day slot the start of the next day, so two slots would share one start: coverage ending at the skipped slot would convert to milliseconds that read back as the next slot, a confirmed gap over a day never fetched. G18 decides that slot's start. G18 (b) is stated on starts, not on dates: `startOf(k)` is G6's instant, unless that instant is not before `startOf(k + 1)`, and then it is one millisecond before `startOf(k + 1)`. That makes `startOf` strictly increasing whatever the zone data says, by construction and not by a list of known dates (the rule looks at most two slots ahead, since an offset is less than a day). In today's zone data it applies exactly to the slot of a skipped date: in Node 26.13 (tzdata 2026e) the forward moves of 23 hours or more in the 418 zones between 1800 and 2040 are ten, each of exactly 24 hours at local midnight (Asia/Manila, Pacific/Guam, Pacific/Saipan, Pacific/Palau and Pacific/Kosrae at the end of 1844, Pacific/Kwajalein in 1993, Pacific/Kiritimati and Pacific/Enderbury at the end of 1994, Pacific/Apia and Pacific/Fakaofo at the end of 2011), and G6's starts collide only on the ten dates those moves skip, since a shorter move keeps them apart. A forward move of 25 hours or of 24 hours at local noon skips no whole date and still makes two G6 starts change order or collide; no zone has one, but an injected rule set can, and the rule on starts covers it. The slot of a skipped date holds a point only if a put stamps one at that synthetic instant, which no clock showed. Whether a record written under other zone data can bring a bar into it depends on G8: under G8 (d) it cannot, because a record's points apply only when the writer's zone data gives their slots the same starts; under G8 (a), (b) and (c) it can (§5.6). A skipped date affects only day slots: a week, month, quarter or year whose first local date was skipped starts at G6's next existing instant, still before the next unit. A local date that exists twice (a zone that moved the other way) makes its slot about 48 hours long, which breaks nothing.
- **`floorSlot(t)`**, the slot at or before `t`: start from the candidate slot of `t`'s UTC date and step until `startOf(k) <= t < startOf(k + 1)`. A day slot's start lies from about 14 hours before to about 36 hours after the UTC midnight of its date with today's offsets (`at` up to 23:59, offsets from UTC−12 to UTC+14), and within a day before to two days after with any offset under a day, historical local mean time included (about −16 hours in Asia/Manila before 1845), so the answer is at most one slot above the candidate and two below it: with `{ unit: "day", zone: "America/Los_Angeles", at: "18:00" }` in winter, local date D starts at D+1 02:00 UTC, so for `t` at D+1 00:30 UTC the candidate D+1 and the slot D both start after `t`, and the answer is D−1. For longer units one step suffices. The build asserts the bound in its tests (test 2) rather than looping without one.
- **Everything else as today, in terms of the two:** `isAligned(t)` is `Number.isSafeInteger(t) && startOf(floorSlot(t)) === t`, and false when `floorSlot` throws `InvalidRangeError` for a `t` outside the domain (below); `snapOut`, `snapIn`, `slotAtOrAfter` and `toMs` keep their code. N1 (outward snap of reads), N13 (inward snap of a put's range) and the watermark (`slotAtOrAfter`, §2.4) keep their meaning, with "the grid point at or before" read on the calendar.
- **Supported domain (G12), checked once, inside the two primitives.** The domain is the slots `kMin` to `kMax` whose local date lies in the years 1–9999; both bounds follow from the descriptor by calendar arithmetic, with no `Intl` call. `floorSlot(t)` first checks `t` against an arithmetic outer bound (UTC 0001-01-01 minus two days to 10000-01-01 plus two days, wider than any zone offset and far inside `Date`'s range of ±8.64 × 10^15 ms), throwing `InvalidRangeError` before any `Intl` call, then steps to its slot and throws `InvalidRangeError` if that slot lies outside `kMin`–`kMax`. `startOf(k)` throws `InvalidRangeError` for a `k` more than two slots outside `kMin`–`kMax` (its neighbours are needed by `floorSlot`'s steps at the edges; their local dates lie within two units of the years 1–9999, from year −1 to year 10001 at most, as for a yearly grid's `kMin − 2` and `kMax + 2`, all inside `Date`'s range and valid for `Intl`). No caller checks the domain itself; every one inherits the primitives' error. `snapOut`, `snapIn`, `slotAtOrAfter` and `toMs` reject with `InvalidRangeError`, as §4.1 says they reject slots beyond the safe integers today; `isAligned` turns it into false, so a put reports `PutError` `'misaligned'`; `get`, `invalidate`, `coverage` and `setFinalizedUntil` reject with `InvalidRangeError` (`engine/cache.ts:345`, `:453`, `:459`, `:481`); a load's stored watermark is set aside as not applied (`storedWatermark`, `engine/restore.ts:201-209`, catches `InvalidRangeError` only); and `hydrate` rejects a snapshot's watermark with `TscacheError` (`checkMeta`, `engine/snapshot.ts:194-199`, which also catches `InvalidRangeError` only). Without the check inside the primitives, every one of those paths would meet `Intl`'s own `RangeError` ("Invalid time value", thrown for any instant beyond ±8.64 × 10^15 ms, such as 9 × 10^15, a safe integer) and rethrow it, so a malformed stored watermark would fail the whole load. A `RangeError` out of `Intl` can then only be a bug. The other checks that assume a safe-integer slot has a timestamp (`grid.ts:107-116`, `:153-157`; `orchestrator/orchestrator.ts:75-77`; `segment/payload.ts:54`) ask the grid for `kMin` and `kMax` instead.

`grid.ts` stays the one place where milliseconds become slots (G13 a): the calendar arithmetic, the `Intl` lookup and the tables of slot starts are in it, and the fixed-grid functions keep their current arithmetic unchanged.

### 5.3 What stays in slots, unchanged

Coverage, segments, the gap rule (N14: "more than K consecutive slots"), the slot cap (N12), pages (N15), the in-flight subtraction (§4.9.1), the persisted page keys and the IndexedDB layout (one database at version 1, keys `['cacheId', 'page']`, N51). A calendar slot is a session, a week or a month, so their sizes read in those units: with the default `segmentSlotCap` of 32,768, one page of a daily cache spans about 89 years, and of a monthly cache about 2,730. That is also true of a fixed daily cache today.

### 5.4 Trading days, weekends and holidays

Every calendar day is a slot (G5 a). A day without a session is an empty slot: the fetcher's response covers it (its `range` is the request) and holds no bar for it, so the engine records a confirmed gap and never fetches it again, exactly as it does today for a weekend on a fixed daily grid. A weekend is two empty slots and a long weekend three or four, within the default K = 4, so they stay inside one segment; a closure of more than four days splits it (N14), as on a fixed daily grid today: a week-long national holiday such as the Shanghai exchange's in October, the Lunar New Year closures, or the New York Stock Exchange's closure from 11 to 16 September 2001 (six empty slots with the weekend). The empty slots inside a segment cost their capacity (weekends alone are about 2/7 of a daily cache's slots). No holiday list is part of the grid, so the numbering never changes when an exchange adds a holiday.

### 5.5 Where the grid is named

- **Config and N20.** `interval` is compared by value: two numbers with `===` as today, two descriptors by their resolved fields, and a number never equals a descriptor. `alignmentOffset` is 0 for a calendar cache. The structural list of N20 (`engine/engine.ts:31-40`) is unchanged; the cache record's list (`engine/restore.ts:26-32`) is compared with the same value equality.
- **Errors.** An unknown unit, a zone the runtime does not resolve, an `at` that is not `HH:MM` from `00:00` to `23:59`, a `weekStart` on a unit other than week, or a non-zero `alignmentOffset` with a calendar interval reject `cache()` with `ConfigError` naming the field. A misaligned put names the calendar ("not the start of a day in America/New_York at 09:30") instead of `t ≡ offset (mod interval)`.
- **Payload, snapshot and cache record (G9 a).** A calendar cache writes `format: 2` envelopes, payloads and cache records, in which one field, `grid`, replaces `interval` and `alignmentOffset`: `grid` is the resolved descriptor. With G8 (d), a format 2 envelope (a snapshot, and so the snapshot of each page record) of a calendar in a zone other than `"UTC"` also stores `starts`: the start of every slot the check covers (G20 says which of the slots the record names those are), as runs `[first, last, shift]` in one canonical form, which a reader compares slot by slot with its own zone data and refuses the record or snapshot on any difference (§5.6). Payloads and the cache record gain nothing beyond `grid` (the cache record's only instant is the watermark, which is not a slot), and the JSON form carries `starts` as arrays of numbers. The payload's rule becomes "slot i is the i-th slot after the slot of `start`", which is what the decoder already computes (`segment/payload.ts:51-57`). A fixed cache keeps writing `format: 1` with the same bytes as today, so no stored record or rendered snapshot of a fixed cache changes. Readers accept both formats; a fixed cache refuses a format 2 snapshot and a calendar cache a format 1 one (the grid differs), and 0.1.0 engines refuse format 2 as an unsupported format (`engine/snapshot.ts:135`, `segment/payload.ts:27`), never misreading it.
- **The JSON form for server rendering (G9 a, N38).** `encodeSnapshot` and `decodeSnapshot` carry both formats: a format 2 envelope and its payloads keep their `grid` (the descriptor is plain JSON) and the base64 of masks and field data, and `decodeSnapshot` checks a format 2 envelope's `grid` for shape (an object with a string `unit`) and leaves its meaning to `hydrate`, as it does for everything else today (`snapshot.ts:122-127`). `decodePayload` rebuilds each payload in its own format instead of always `format: 1` (`snapshot.ts:107-119`). With G8 (d) the JSON form carries a format 2 envelope's `starts` as it is, an array of triples of numbers, and `decodeSnapshot` checks its shape as it checks every other array today, position by position, holes included: `starts`, when present, must be an array whose every position holds an array of exactly three numbers, and anything else (`starts: 5`, a hole, a pair, a triple with a string) throws `TscacheError` and nothing else. A missing `starts` is carried as missing. Whether one is required, what its triples mean, their order and their canonical form are `hydrate`'s to check (§5.6), like the meaning of every other field. `EncodedSnapshot` and `EncodedSegmentPayload` become unions of the two formats, as `Snapshot` and `DenseSegmentPayload` do. A 0.1.0 `decodeSnapshot` refuses a format 2 value ("format must be 1"), so a page on the old library given a new server's snapshot fails at decoding, before `hydrate`.
- **Fetch request (G10 a).** `FetchRequest.interval` mirrors the config: `number | CalendarInterval`; `alignmentOffset` is 0 for a calendar cache. The range is in milliseconds, aligned to slot starts, coalesced as today: a miss that abuts covered slots is extended one slot into them (a month on a monthly cache), and a flank with no coverage next to it is not (`orchestrator/orchestrator.ts:126-157`). The comment at `types.ts:333` changes from "flanks extended by one interval" to say that.
- **Results and events.** Unchanged in shape: `timestamps`, `coverage`, `misses`, snapshot ranges, watermarks and M7's `cacheChanged` ranges are milliseconds on the grid, converted by `toMs` as today.

### 5.6 Zone data that differs between writer and reader

A slot's start in a zone is computed from the runtime's zone data, and two engines can hold different data: a browser updated between two visits, which reads the records an older version of itself wrote, or a Node server and the browser that hydrates its snapshot. Call them the _writer_ and the _reader_. The descriptor is the same in both, so N20, `hydrate` and a load all accept the grid. The difference need not be recent, large or known. Measured on one machine on 2026-11-16, over the daily 09:30 starts of 24 zones from 1900 to 2026: Chromium 156 agrees with Node 26.13 (tzdata 2026e) on every day, and Bun 1.4.2 (ICU 78.1) differs from Node on one, 1965-03-31 in America/Sao_Paulo, whose slot starts at 12:30 UTC in Node and at 11:30 UTC in Bun (the two end that summer time a day apart). G8 decides what a reader checks. This section states the problem, what each option guarantees and what it leaves open, so that no option claims more than it checks.

**What a record says.** A snapshot or page record is written from slots and read back into slots through instants. Its _anchors_ are stored as instants: the two ends of its `range`, the two ends of each coverage range, and each payload's `start`. Everything else is relative: entry `i` of a payload is the `i`-th slot after the slot of its `start` (`segment/payload.ts:51-57`). The record's _named slots_ are the slots of the two ends of its range, of its coverage ranges, and of each payload (`count` slots from its first); they are every slot the record says anything about. A put supplies instants, so the bar in slot `k` was stamped at the writer's `startOf(k)`, and a covered slot without a bar says that no bar exists at that instant. The watermark is an instant too, but names no slot: every reader places it with its own `slotAtOrAfter`.

**When it means the same.** If every named slot starts at the same instant under the reader's zone data as under the writer's, each anchor reads as the slot the writer meant, so coverage and points land in the writer's slots, and every timestamp, coverage endpoint and miss the reader returns for them is the instant the writer would return. The reader then holds what the writer held. If a named slot `k` starts at different instants, the two engines disagree about that slot, in one of two ways:

- **Class 1, within a slot.** The writer's start of `k` is not a slot start for the reader: a daylight-saving date or an offset differs, as for São Paulo above. Kept in slot `k`, the bar reads at the reader's start of `k`, an hour (or whatever the difference is) from the instant it was put at.
- **Class 2, to another slot.** The writer's start of `k` is the reader's start of a different slot. That takes a difference of a whole slot, so it happens on day grids only: two readings of one local time are less than 48 hours apart, since every offset is less than a day (the largest in today's data is under 16 hours). The cause is a move across the date line that one data set lacks or dates differently, which adds, removes or moves a skipped local date or a repeated one. Read through an anchor on such a slot, every later point lands one session away. Read relative to an anchor both agree on, the bar stays in slot `k` although its instant now belongs to a neighbour: the slot of a date the reader skips can hold a bar, and a date that exists for the reader can be left covered without one, a confirmed gap that is never fetched.

A difference that leaves every named slot's start unchanged, because it lies outside the record or at a time of day no start uses, changes nothing in the record.

**What a reader can detect.** The reader has its own zone data and the record. It knows of the writer's starts only what the record stores:

| The record stores                 | The reader can check                       | It sees                            |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Its anchors, as today             | That each anchor is a slot start           | Class 1 at an anchor's slot        |
| Also each anchor's slot index     | That each anchor is the start of that slot | Any difference at an anchor's slot |
| Also the start of each named slot | Each of those starts                       | Every difference                   |

Nothing less than the last row is complete. Zone data can differ at a single named slot (São Paulo above) or at two neighbours (a skipped date dated a day apart, finding R2-1 of `docs/reviews/step-118.md`) and agree everywhere else, so a check that does not compare the start of slot `k` cannot know whether it differs; any rule that inspects fewer slots has a residual of that shape, whatever its wording. The complete check costs one slot start per named slot. A stamp naming the version of the zone data would be cheaper, but the runtime offers none where records are read: Node has `process.versions.tz` (`"2026e"`), Bun 1.4.2 reports only its ICU version, and Chromium 156 exposes nothing (no property of `Intl`, of a formatter's `resolvedOptions()`, of `Temporal` or of `navigator` names it, `Intl.supportedValuesOf` accepts no such key, and ECMA-402 defines none).

**The options (G8).** Each option applies a record under the condition in its second column and refuses it otherwise. **What (d) leaves open, exactly.** Inside the record, what G20 decides: nothing when every named slot is checked, and with G20 (c) the slots strictly inside a gap (below). Outside the record:

1. Zone data that differs outside the record's named slots is not seen, and does not matter to the record.
2. A watermark is placed by the reader's own `slotAtOrAfter`, as today, so the first volatile slot can differ from the writer's (§9.1, case k). It cannot reach a covered slot of a record that applies: that slot's start is the writer's, which is before the watermark.
3. The check compares two sets of zone data and does not know which is right. A bar stamped under zone data that both engines share, and that a later release corrects, stays where it was put until an engine with the corrected data refuses the record.
4. It guards records and snapshots, not memory: it assumes that one engine sees one set of zone data from start to stop. Whether a runtime that takes its zone data from the operating system can change it under a running worker was not verified.
5. `starts` is part of the record, which is untrusted input (§4.10): a forged record can carry starts that agree with the reader, which makes it a valid record and nothing more. Every check of §4.5 and §4.10.5 applies as before, and a malformed `starts` is refused like any malformed field.

**(d) in detail.** The _nominal start_ of slot `k` is its first local date at `at`, read as if the zone were UTC: calendar arithmetic on the descriptor, the same in every runtime. The slot's _shift_ is `startOf(k)` minus its nominal start: 18,000,000 ms for a New York winter day, 14,400,000 in summer. `starts` is an array of runs `[first, last, shift]`: the slots `first` to `last` are checked slots, and each has that shift. The _checked slots_ are the named slots with G20 (a) or (b), and with G20 (c), recommended, the named slots less those strictly inside a gap (below); every anchor's slot is a checked slot under each. `starts` has one canonical form, so that equal states have equal records (§4.10.2, "Consequences the tests rely on") and a comparison of two records is determined: the runs are ascending, disjoint and maximal, which means that a run ends only where the next checked slot is not the next slot or has another shift. A writer writes that form. A reader refuses any other as malformed, two runs that could be one as much as runs out of order, so it never has a record to rewrite into the form. A slot that starts as G18 (b) says has a shift of its own, so it is a run of one. The writer builds the runs from the tables of §5.7 when it makes a snapshot or a page record. The reader first runs today's checks (§4.5, rules 1 to 5), which give it the named slots, and so the checked slots, as it reads the anchors. It then requires that `starts` is canonical, that its runs cover exactly those checked slots, and that `startOf(k)` equals the nominal start plus the run's shift for every slot `k` of every run, stopping at the first that does not. Both directions hold: if every checked slot starts where the writer had it, every anchor reads as the writer's slot and every run matches; if one does not, either an anchor reads as another slot, and the runs no longer cover the reader's checked slots, or a start fails its comparison. A calendar in `"UTC"` stores no `starts` and needs no check, since its starts are arithmetic in every runtime.

**A payload's slots outside coverage are checked too.** A snapshot's payloads can hold points outside its coverage: the provisional tail at or after its watermark, which `hydrate` does not apply (N35), or points before a coverage range (§9.1, case j). Their slots are named slots and, as payload slots, checked slots under every option of G20. A difference at a tail's slot therefore refuses a snapshot whose covered part would have applied, although `hydrate` would have dropped the tail anyway. That is intended, for three reasons: a payload's entries are placed from its first slot, which can itself lie outside coverage; a snapshot is refused whole and never clipped (N35, N69), so a reader does not pick the part it agrees with; and the tail's points are part of what the snapshot holds, which a reader of columns from a snapshot (the later option N38 leaves room for) would return at its own instants. The price is a refetch of what the snapshot covered, in the case where two engines differ at the forming bar and nowhere before it. Checking only a payload's first slot and its slots inside coverage would be as exact for what `hydrate` applies; it is not proposed, and would have to be chosen before the format ships, since it changes what `starts` holds.

**Cost of (d).** Measured in Node 26.13 with `formatToParts`, tables cold, for daily New York slots at 09:30: one year, 3 runs, 1.6 ms; 35 years, 71 runs, 54 ms; a whole page of 32,768 slots (1970 to 2059), 180 runs, 145 ms, which is 4.4 µs per slot. Those 180 runs are 540 numbers beside about 1.3 MB of field data for five float64 fields; a zone without daylight saving (Asia/Tokyo since 1970) is one run. These are the starts that a first read of the same slots computes anyway (§5.7), so the check moves that cost to the load. With the tables warm, comparing 32,768 starts takes 0.5 ms, and the tables are shared by every cache of a descriptor (G19), so a thousand caches of New York sessions pay the 145 ms once; a full daily page uses 64 of the 256 tables. The cost follows the slots that are checked, not the points.

**The load's bound, and which slots are checked (G20).** N46 bounds a load: `cache()` of a persisted cache resolves after its records are applied, at most 5 s after the open (`timeoutMs`, N61; architecture §4.10.6). The timer bounds the store's opening and its read. The records are then applied in one synchronous call, `restore` (§4.10.5), which no timer interrupts and which today takes time in proportion to the stored points; `hydrate` and `snapshot` are synchronous too (§4.6). Checking every named slot breaks that proportion, because coverage costs nothing to hold and one slot start per slot to check. A daily New York cache that was once read from the year 1 holds a confirmed gap of about 739,000 slots (2,023 years, 23 pages) before its first bar. Checking them takes about 3.3 s of synchronous work in the SharedWorker, during which no tab's call is answered, and a gap over the whole domain (3.65 million slots) about 16 s, past N46's bound. They are more slots than the 256 tables of G19 (c) hold (131,072), so every such cache pays in full at every load, and its flush pays the same to write `starts`. A forged snapshot can claim such coverage in a few bytes. G20 decides how the check stays inside the bound:

- **(a) Every named slot, inside the synchronous steps.** Nothing is left open inside the record. The cost is unbounded, as just described, and N46's bound no longer holds for a cache with a long gap.
- **(b) Every named slot, outside the synchronous steps.** The comparison is a pure function of a record and the zone data, so it need not run in the step that applies the record. A load checks its page records before `restore`, one table block (512 slots, about 2 ms cold) per turn of the event loop, newest page first, so other calls are answered in between; at the load's deadline the pages not yet checked count as refused, and `restore` stays one synchronous call (N57). A flush fills in `starts` the same way before its write, under the job's deadline (N61). `hydrate` and `snapshot` cannot yield, so a snapshot may name at most a fixed number of slots (65,536 in the sketch: two daily pages, about 290 ms cold); `snapshot` rejects a range that names more, and `hydrate` refuses such a snapshot. Nothing is left open inside the record. The cost: the persister gains a phase and a rule at the deadline, an amendment of §4.10.5 and §4.10.6; the cache with the 739,000-slot gap spends about 3.3 s of chunked work at every load and at every full flush; a cache that names more cold slots than 5 s check (about 1.1 million) loses its oldest pages at every load and cannot finish a full flush; and a server cannot render a snapshot that names more slots than the limit.
- **(c) The slots that hold data, and the two ends of every gap, within a budget.** A _gap_ here is a maximal stretch of a coverage range that no payload reaches. The checked slots are the two ends of the range, every payload's slots, and the first and last slot of every gap. A gap then costs two slot starts however long it is. Every offset lookup a record asks for is charged against a budget per load, per `hydrate` and per `snapshot`, which the cost model below defines. Every anchor's slot is a checked slot, so coverage and points are in the writer's slots and every point reads at the instant it was put at, as under (a). What it leaves open is a slot strictly inside a gap whose start differs: the reader takes over the writer's "no bar" for that slot, at its own start of it (§9.1, case m). That is wrong only if the upstream holds a bar at an instant that is a slot start for the reader and not for the writer, and no fetch of the writer's returned it; a fetch that returned it would have been rejected whole as `'misaligned'` (§5.5) and would have covered nothing. An instant that is a slot start for both is, for the writer, the start of another slot inside the same gap (the gap's two ends agree and starts are strictly increasing), where the writer found no bar.
- **(d) Runs from the zone's transitions, where the runtime has `Temporal`.** `getTimeZoneTransition` gives the instants at which the offset changes, so a run is known in one step and a gap costs its transitions: the 16,159 transitions of America/New_York from the year 1 to 9999 take 9 ms in Node 26.13. It is complete only if `Temporal` and `Intl` read the same zone data in a runtime, which the library cannot check, and a runtime without `Temporal` falls back to (a), so the bound holds in some runtimes only.
- **(e) As (c), with one budget for all loads that are pending together.** The budget is then an allowance of the engine: a load draws its records' charges from it, newest page first, and the allowance is full again only when no load is pending. However many caches open together, their loads make at most one budget of lookups in total, so N46's 5 s holds for the last of them as for the first. The cost: a charge is a worst case, about three times what a record of bars makes in lookups, so caches opened together use the allowance up early. With 524,288, 23 caches of ten years of daily bars (22,026 each) fit, and a 24th opened with them applies nothing and fetches again, although the first of them took 16 ms and the others read warm tables.

§5 assumes (c). Its bound is one model, with one unit and one inequality, so that no part of the `Intl` work is priced apart from the rest.

**The unit** is the offset lookup: one `Intl` call that gives the zone's offset at an instant. A calendar cache in a zone asks `Intl` for nothing else, and a fixed cache and a `"UTC"` calendar ask it for nothing. The resolution's three lookups are a rule of the build and not an observation: it never makes a fourth. It gives the instant of `Temporal`'s `"compatible"` wherever at most one transition lies within a day of the local time, and it was compared with `Temporal` on the five days around every transition of every zone between 1800 and 2040, at twelve times of day, 1,606,560 resolutions in Node 26.13, without a difference.

**The charge** of a record is computed from its shape alone, with no lookup: the `count` of each payload and the lengths of `segments` and `coverage`.

| For                                    | Charge | It pays for                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Each payload slot (the sum of `count`) | 6      | Its start in the check                                                                                      |
| Each payload                           | 24     | Its `start` as an anchor (12) and the two ends of a gap beside it (12)                                      |
| Each coverage range                    | 48     | Its two anchors (24), the two ends of a gap in it (12), and its two ends converted back for the result (12) |
| The range                              | 36     | Its two anchors (24) and its two ends as checked slots (12)                                                 |
| A watermark                            | 12     | Its conversion                                                                                              |

**The inequality:** for one record, a load, a `hydrate` or a `snapshot` makes at most as many lookups as the record's charge, wherever its slots lie, whatever the tables hold and whatever it claims. It follows term by term from the two tables: every anchor is charged 12 and every checked slot 6, and the checked slots are the payload slots, two for each gap and the two ends of the range, where a record has at most one gap for each payload and one for each coverage range. A `hydrate` into a cache that already covers part of the record reports more ranges than the record has; their extra ends are the reader's own coverage, at 6 lookups each, as for any result that reports ranges.

**The budget** is 524,288 lookups in the sketch, and a record's charge is compared with it before any lookup is made:

- **`hydrate`** computes the charge after rule 1 of its validation, the envelope, which needs no lookup, and before rule 2, the range, which is the first to convert an instant (architecture §4.5). `coverage` and `segments` must be arrays and every payload's `count` a positive safe integer; a charge above the budget fails with `TscacheError`, like any other failure of the validation.
- **`restore`** takes the page records newest first and adds their charges, with 12 for the cache record's watermark. A page that would take the sum past the budget is not applied, nor any older one: they are skipped like invalid pages (rewritten at the next flush), and no lookup is made for them.
- **`snapshot(range)`** computes the charge from the cache's own slots, the payload spans and coverage ranges inside the range, before it converts anything. Above the budget it throws `TscacheError`; `InvalidRangeError` stays what architecture §2.6 says it is, a malformed range. `get(range, { snapshot: true })` above the budget resolves with its read and without a `snapshot`, so the locked partial-result policy (architecture §2.3: `get` never rejects for data-availability reasons) holds, and the application finds the field missing where it would encode it.
- **A flush** builds each page record as `snapshot` does, within that record's charge. A page record whose own charge exceeds the budget is written all the same and skipped by every load; with the default `segmentSlotCap` that takes more than about 10,900 separate coverage ranges on one page.

**In time.** A lookup, with the reading of its parts, measures 2.2 µs in Node 26.13 and up to 2.9 µs where every start is computed alone (the 1.4 µs of §5.7 is the bare `formatToParts` call), so a lone start costs 9 to 15 µs, not the 4.4 µs of a start in a table. A load at the budget therefore takes at most 1.5 s, against N46's 5 s. Measured records, with the reader's lookups counted:

| Record (daily, New York, 09:30)                                              | Charge  | Lookups made | Time   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ------------ | ------ |
| Ten years of bars                                                            | 22,026  | 7,432        | 16 ms  |
| A full page of bars, 89 years                                                | 196,716 | 65,882       | 143 ms |
| One bar in each block of the domain, 7,134 bars (finding R3-1)               | 214,104 | 100,136      | 224 ms |
| 10,921 coverage ranges of one slot, 150 slots apart, at the budget           | 524,244 | 262,566      | 578 ms |
| 6,721 payloads of one slot, each in a coverage range of three, at the budget | 524,274 | 228,773      | 576 ms |

No shape measured made more than 0.51 of its charge in lookups. The 739,000-slot gap adds the charge of its two ends. A daily cache of bars reaches the budget at about 239 years (6 per slot), although such a record makes about a third of its charge in lookups.

**What N46 then guarantees.** The budget is per load. `restore` is synchronous and loads run one after another, as they do for fixed caches today, so N46's 5 s holds for each load taken alone: the store's read, then at most one budget of lookups. Of `k` caches opened together the last waits for the `k − 1` before it, at most `k` budgets in the worst case and, for records of bars, the times of the table above. Option (e) trades that for one budget across a burst.

**What the budget costs.** A cache whose records charge more than the budget loads its newest pages only and fetches the older ones again when they are read; a snapshot that would charge more is not made, and `get` returns its read without one.

**How much a refusal costs.** A difference at one checked slot refuses the whole snapshot or page record, and a daily page is 89 years. That is the price of the simple rule; it is paid only where the two sets of zone data differ on dates the record checks. Most changes to zone data concern future or very recent dates, where an OHLC cache holds little, but data sets also differ about the past, as São Paulo's 1965 shows. Because (d) stores the starts themselves, a later version can apply the slots whose starts agree and fetch only the others, which is exact slot by slot and needs no new format; (e) cannot. That refinement is not part of the first build.

**Server rendering** is the visible case: a Node server and a browser render and hydrate the same descriptor with different zone data. The docs tell server-rendering applications to keep Node's ICU data current, and `hydrate`'s rejection is the signal that the two differ. An engine whose zone data is the outdated one cannot hold the affected bars by fetching either: a put of a bar stamped at an instant that its data does not make a slot start is rejected as `'misaligned'` (§5.5), under every option.

### 5.7 Performance

The fixed grid's hot loops are untouched: `isAligned` and `slotOf` on put (`engine/batch.ts:133`, `:297`) and `msOf` on read (`engine/read.ts:83`) keep their arithmetic, and the grid kind is decided once per call, not per point, so the bench gate (`docs/benchmarking.md`) holds for fixed caches with no budget change. A calendar grid in a non-UTC zone needs `formatToParts` to compute a slot's start, two or three calls of about 1.4 µs each for the slot's G6 instant and as many for the next slot's, which G18 (b) compares it with (measured in Node 26.13; §5.6 has the cost model), and every conversion asks for starts, not only stored data: `snapOut` of each read's and invalidation's endpoints, `toMs` of coverage and misses, `slotAtOrAfter` of each watermark, and `floorSlot`'s neighbours. With G19 (c), the engine keeps **tables of slot starts per resolved descriptor**: a table holds the starts of 512 consecutive slots (a block, `⌊k / 512⌋`), computed whole under the fill rule below, except at the two edges of the domain: a block that reaches past `kMin − 2` or `kMax + 2`, the margin `startOf` accepts (§5.2), holds only the slots within it, and the rest are never computed (for a yearly grid, year 9999 is slot 8,029 in the block of slots 7,680–8,191, whose table holds 7,680–8,031), and all tables of the engine share one least-recently-used order with a fixed capacity (256 tables, about 1 MB, in the sketch). The tables are a pure function of the descriptor and the runtime's zone rules, so every cache with the same descriptor shares them: a thousand daily caches of New York sessions use the same tables, and `clear`, `clearAll` and eviction have nothing to drop. **The fill rule:** a table is computed whole only for a conversion that needs more than half of its slots at once (of the slots an edge table would hold), and every other start is computed alone, in at most six offset lookups (two G6 resolutions, §5.6), and not kept. A read converting the slots it returns, a put converting its timestamps and the check of G8 (d) converting a record's checked slots each know their slots before they convert them, so each counts what it needs of a block before it computes anything; the ends of a range, a watermark and `floorSlot`'s neighbours are lone starts. A table that is held answers every lookup. A table takes one resolution per slot and one more for the slot after its last, so no conversion makes more than two G6 resolutions for each start it needs, wherever its slots lie and whatever the tables hold. Computing a block whole on first touch would break that: a record with one bar in each block of the daily domain, 7,134 bars, costs 15.9 s that way and 224 ms under the rule, its anchors included (finding R3-1; §5.6 has the measurement). Costs: a cold block costs about 2.2 ms (measured: 145 ms for the 64 blocks of a daily page); a 35-year daily chart touches 25 blocks, about 50 ms once, then one array lookup per point on every redraw; a batch that needs less than half of a block, such as monthly marks on a daily grid, pays four to six lookups per point on every conversion; the capacity covers about 350 years of daily slots or 10,000 years of monthly slots before a single read can make tables evict one another. A UTC calendar needs no table: its conversions are arithmetic. The build step adds calendar rows to the bench (put and read of a year and of 35 years of daily bars in `America/New_York`, cold and warm tables, and a read of more blocks than the capacity) with budgets set from their first measurement. With G8 (d), a load or a `hydrate` computes the start of every slot it checks (§5.6; with G20 (c), the slots that hold data and the ends of gaps), so it fills the tables of those blocks before any read does: 64 blocks, about 145 ms measured, for a full daily page of bars, once per descriptor. M3 counts segment bytes only and leaves object overhead out (E5 a); the tables' fixed capacity, engine-wide, limits their memory.

### 5.8 The page side

The page needs grid arithmetic too: M7's integrations decide whether a change touches a view by snapping the view's range outward (`integrations/shared/changes.ts:40-49`), and the reference clients compare ranges against half an interval (`reference/shared/src/chart/view.ts:43-53`), look one interval past the visible end (`reference/shared/src/session.ts:301`, `:408`) and floor `now` to the forming bar (`reference/shared/src/market.ts:42-47`). All of them assume a fixed step. A new entry, `@anishbenji/tscache/grid` (G11 a), exports the outward snap and the slot start and length in milliseconds for both kinds of interval, built from the same `grid.ts`:

```ts
// @anishbenji/tscache/grid (sketch)
export function snapRange(
  range: Range,
  interval: number | CalendarInterval,
  alignmentOffset?: number,
): Range;
export function slotStart(
  t: number,
  interval: number | CalendarInterval,
  alignmentOffset?: number,
): number;
export function nextSlotStart(
  t: number,
  interval: number | CalendarInterval,
  alignmentOffset?: number,
): number;
```

An application that never imports it pays nothing; the integrations import it and replace `snappedOut`, and the reference clients replace `interval / 2` with half the length of the slot at the range's edge, `+ market.interval` with `nextSlotStart`, and `formingBar` with `slotStart`.

### 5.9 Old workers and the handshake

A page with calendar support can meet a worker without it: a 0.1.0 SharedWorker at a fixed URL, until M5's generation key (F1 a) gives each library version its own worker. Such a worker rejects a calendar config with `ConfigError` ("interval must be a positive integer, got [object Object]"), loudly but confusingly, because the descriptor sits in `interval` (G4 a) and `resolveCacheConfig` rejects a non-number there (`engine/validate.ts:17-21`, `:118`). With G14 (a), a worker that supports calendars lists `'calendar-grid'` in `hello.features` (N79, from M7), and the client refuses a calendar config itself with a clear `ConfigError` when the worker does not list it. `PROTOCOL_VERSION` stays 1 (N37): no op is added, and the `cache` op's parameters widen.

## 6. Interactions

### 6.1 M3, memory bounds (`docs/design/m3-memory.md`, decided as decision 67)

- **What is counted** is segment bytes at capacity (E5 a), not slots, so a calendar cache is counted like any other. Its empty weekend slots cost capacity and are counted, as on a fixed daily grid.
- **The eviction unit** is the page (E6 a), `segmentSlotCap` slots. For a daily or longer calendar cache one page spans decades (§5.3), so eviction can drop such a cache only whole unless the application lowers `segmentSlotCap`. That is already true of a fixed daily cache; the M3 guide's figures ("17 pages per year at one minute") gain a sentence for calendar caches.
- **The tables of slot starts** are object overhead, outside the count (E5 a). Their engine-wide capacity bounds them whatever the queries and however many caches share a descriptor (G19, §5.7); they hold no points, so neither an eviction nor a `clear` touches them.
- Nothing in M3's eviction, pinning or persistence rules depends on the length of a slot.

### 6.2 M7, change notifications (`docs/design/m7-change-events.md`, decided as decision 65; built on step 114, `feat/114-m7-change-events`)

- **Event ranges** are milliseconds on the grid, produced by `toMs` in the engine, so they are correct on a calendar grid with no M7 change.
- **The integrations' outward snap** (`integrations/shared/changes.ts:40-49`, `snappedOut`) computes `floor` and `ceil` with fixed-step arithmetic from `{ interval, alignmentOffset }`. On a calendar grid it is wrong at every view edge, so a change at the edge of a view would be missed or a change outside it would wake the view. The calendar build step replaces it with `snapRange` from the grid entry (§5.8) and widens the integrations' `Grid` type to the config's `interval`.
- **N84** (reading after a change) is unaffected: it decides between a cache-only and a fetching read from the event's lists, not from slot lengths.
- **`hello.features`** (N79) carries the calendar capability (G14).

### 6.3 M5, deploy skew (`docs/design/m5-deploy-skew.md`, decided as decision 71)

- **A deploy that changes a cache's grid** (a fixed interval to a calendar one, or one descriptor to another) is a structural change. M5's generation key holds the library version and the fetcher URL, not cache configs (§4.2 of that proposal), so while a worker of the same generation runs, a new tab's `cache()` meets N20 and rejects with `ConfigError`, as it does today for any structural change. The application's remedies are today's: a new cache id for the new grid, or a fetcher URL that changes with the deploy (a new generation).
- **Persisted records under the old grid** are not applied by a cache of the new grid (structure mismatch, §4.10.2) and are rewritten at its next flush. A rolled-back deploy (a 0.1.0 engine) refuses the format 2 records as an unsupported format and rewrites its own.
- **A library upgrade that brings calendar support** starts a new worker under F1 (a), so an old worker never receives a calendar config from a new page once M5 is built. Before M5, G14 catches it (§5.9).
- **A change of zone data** is not a deploy: a browser update brings it, and the newer version then reads the records the older one wrote. §5.6 covers that case; zone data that changes under a running worker is its point 4, an assumption.

### 6.4 Versioning and release

All additive at runtime: a widened `interval` type in the config, a new entry (`./grid`), new snapshot and record formats written only by calendar caches (with their JSON form), and a new feature name. Compile-time breaks for TypeScript code that reads the grid back, fixed-grid applications included, because the types widen whatever grid a cache uses: `CacheConfig.interval` (a config the application reads back, as `reference/shared/src/market.ts:44-46` destructures and divides it), `ResolvedCacheConfig.interval` (returned by `Engine.cache()` and `configOf()` and exported from `./engine`, `engine/engine.ts:117`, `:140`; `entries/engine.ts:39`) and `FetchRequest.interval` become `number | CalendarInterval`, so arithmetic on them must narrow first; and the four public types that carry a grid, `Snapshot`, `DenseSegmentPayload`, `EncodedSnapshot` and `EncodedSegmentPayload` (`entries/index.ts:31-34`), become unions of their format 1 and format 2 shapes, so code that reads their `interval` or `alignmentOffset` narrows on `format` first. The compiler does not catch every use: `String(request.interval)`, as the documented fetchers build their query (`site/snippets/fetcher.ts:50`, `site/snippets/fetcher-columns.ts:44`), compiles for both kinds and sends `interval=[object Object]` for a calendar cache; those snippets and the fetcher guide gain a calendar branch in the build step. Under 0.x semver that is a minor release (`packages/tscache/README.md`, "Stability"); the release notes name all seven types and the `String(interval)` pattern. G17 places it in its own minor release, after M3 and before M5, in the order of decision 62.

## 7. Migration

- **Fixed-grid users:** nothing changes at runtime. Their configs resolve as today, their records and snapshots keep `format: 1` byte for byte, their fetch requests carry the same numbers, and their hot paths keep their arithmetic (§5.7). At compile time, TypeScript code that does arithmetic on `interval` read from a `CacheConfig`, a `ResolvedCacheConfig` or a `FetchRequest`, or reads it from one of the four snapshot and payload types, narrows it first (§6.4); this repository's reference client is such code (`reference/shared/src/market.ts:44-46`, `session.ts:301`, `:408`).
- **Users of the daily-by-date recipe** who move to `{ unit: "day", zone, at }`: the timestamps change meaning, from dates at 00:00 UTC to session opens. Use a new cache id. The structural change already keeps the old records from loading into the new cache; a new id also keeps tabs of the old deploy, which still pass the old config, from rejecting each other under N20. The fetcher stops restamping bars.
- **Users of the monthly ordinal recipe** who move to `{ unit: "month" }`: timestamps change from ordinals to milliseconds. Use a new cache id; the fetcher and the chart drop their conversions.
- **Users of the weekly recipe in UTC** need not move: a fixed week with an offset is the same set of grid points. A calendar week is a different config (and numbers its slots from another week, §5.2), so moving means a new id for no gain. Weekly bars that start in a local zone move to `{ unit: "week", zone }`.
- **Records under an abandoned id** stay on disk until the application clears them with `clearAll`, as for any cache id an application stops using.
- **The docs** (G16 b): `site/concepts/grid.md` replaces the daily-DST and monthly recipes with the calendar grid, keeps the weekly recipe for UTC weeks and the jitter recipe, and keeps the ordinal technique as a note for calendars the built-in units do not cover; the snippet regions and their `docs:run` checks follow.

## 9. Contract tests for the later step

Engine-logic tests (Codex writes them first, from the approved text), at the level of `grid.ts`, the config, the put and read paths, snapshots and records. Zone tests use instants whose rules are settled (New York in 2026: daylight saving from 8 March to 1 November) and never the current date (`docs/testing.md`), and the property tests run against an injected offset function as well as the real `Intl`, so they do not depend on one runtime's ICU data.

1. **Fixed grids unchanged.** Every existing `grid.ts` contract test passes as it is; a fixed cache's payloads, snapshots and records are equal, field for field, to those of 0.1.0 (`format: 1`, `interval`, `alignmentOffset`).
2. **Calendar primitives, per unit.** For random slots and instants over the domain, in UTC and in the zones of tests 3, 4 and 6: `startOf` strictly increasing; `floorSlot(startOf(k)) === k`; `floorSlot(t) === k` exactly when `startOf(k) <= t < startOf(k + 1)`; leap years, month lengths, year ends and negative slots (before 1970) included. Slot 0 of each unit is the unit containing 1970-01-01 (§5.2), with `weekStart` 1 and 7. `floorSlot` never steps more than once above or twice below its candidate (a spy on the step count), with the extreme case `{ unit: "day", zone: "America/Los_Angeles", at: "18:00" }` at 00:30 UTC in winter, whose answer is two slots below the candidate (§5.2).
3. **A zone's daily session.** `{ unit: "day", zone: "America/New_York", at: "09:30" }`: 2026-03-06 starts at 14:30 UTC and 2026-03-09 at 13:30 UTC, both aligned; 14:30 UTC on 2026-03-09 is not.
4. **Transitions (G6, G18).** `at: "02:30"` on 2026-03-08 in New York starts at 03:30 local (07:30 UTC); `at: "01:30"` on 2026-11-01 starts at the earlier instant (05:30 UTC). A zone with a 30-minute shift (`Australia/Lord_Howe`), a zone with a 45-minute offset (`Asia/Kathmandu`) and a southern-hemisphere zone keep the properties of test 2. Skipped dates: in `Pacific/Apia`, the day slots of 2011-12-29, 2011-12-30 and 2011-12-31 have three distinct, increasing starts, the skipped 2011-12-30 starting as G18 decides (with G18 (b), one millisecond before 2011-12-31's); the same for `Pacific/Kwajalein` over 1993-08-20 to 1993-08-22. Coverage that ends at the skipped slot converts with `toMs` and snaps back to that slot, not the next; a fetch over it records the slot as a gap; and a put of a bar stamped at the next day's start lands in the next day's slot. With injected rules that move the clock forward at local noon, by 25 hours and by 24 hours (no whole date is skipped, and two G6 starts change order or collide), `startOf` is still strictly increasing and `floorSlot(startOf(k)) === k` (G18 (b) is stated on starts, §5.2).
5. **Domain guard, in the primitives.** For safe integers outside the domain (the first instant after the year 9999, 8.64 × 10^15 + 1 and 9 × 10^15, beyond `Date`'s range), with a spy on the offset seam that must not be called: `floorSlot` and `startOf` throw `InvalidRangeError`; a put with such a timestamp rejects with `PutError` `'misaligned'`; `get` and `invalidate` over a range ending there and `setFinalizedUntil` at it reject with `InvalidRangeError`; `hydrate` of a snapshot whose `finalizedUntil` is there rejects with `TscacheError`; and a load whose stored cache record holds that `finalizedUntil` neither rejects nor applies the watermark, and the cache rewrites its records at the next flush (§4.10.5).
6. **Weeks and months in a zone.** A London week starts on Monday at local midnight in both seasons; a month in a zone starts at `at` on its first local day; quarters and years likewise.
7. **Snaps and watermark.** `snapOut`, `snapIn`, `slotAtOrAfter` and `toMs` on calendar grids keep §4.1's contracts: outward never narrower, inward never wider, `start === end` one slot when aligned and two otherwise, a watermark mid-month making the next month's start the volatile boundary.
8. **Domain (G12).** Ranges and watermarks beyond the years 1–9999 reject with `InvalidRangeError`. For each unit, in UTC and in a non-UTC zone (`America/New_York`) with the tables of G19: the first and last slot of the domain, `kMin` and `kMax` (year 9999 is slot 8,029 of a yearly grid), convert exactly both ways; `startOf(kMax + 3)` and `startOf(kMin − 3)` throw `InvalidRangeError`; and a spy on the offset seam sees no call for a slot outside `kMin − 2` to `kMax + 2`, although the edge blocks extend past it (§5.7).
9. **Config.** Each invalid descriptor of §5.5 rejects with `ConfigError` naming its field; a calendar `interval` with a non-zero `alignmentOffset` rejects; the resolved descriptor is frozen with its defaults; N20: equal descriptors join one cache, a differing unit, zone, `at` or `weekStart` rejects, two aliases of one zone reject (G7), and a number never equals a descriptor.
10. **Put.** A misaligned timestamp rejects the batch with `'misaligned'`, its index and value, and a message naming the calendar; aligned batches store their points at the right slots.
11. **Read.** Timestamps come back as slot starts; coverage and misses are milliseconds on the calendar; a read's ranges equal `toMs` of the snapped slots.
12. **Segments.** On a daily calendar, a weekend (2 empty slots) and a 4-day gap stay inside one segment and a 5-day gap splits it (N14); pages are `segmentSlotCap` calendar slots (N15).
13. **Snapshots and zone data (G9, G8).** A calendar cache's snapshot has `format: 2` and its `grid`; it round-trips through `hydrate`; a fixed cache refuses it and a calendar cache refuses a fixed one; a differing descriptor is refused. Zone data that differs between writer and reader is injected as two offset functions, the writer's for the engine that makes the record and the reader's for the engine that receives it. Each case of §9.1 runs twice, as a snapshot hydrated and as page records loaded, and has the outcome its table gives for the G8 option the user takes, so the table also pins what that option leaves open. With G8 (d), recommended, and the checked slots G20 gives: a record applies exactly in the cases where no checked slot's start differs, and a read of the record's range from the reader's cache then equals the same read from the writer's, in timestamps, field values, coverage and misses (a read that reaches past the record's range can differ, as in cases k and m); in every other case `hydrate` rejects with `TscacheError` and changes nothing, and a load skips the page, applies nothing of it and rewrites it at the next flush. Also with G8 (d): a snapshot or page record whose `starts` is missing, is not an array of triples of safe integers, is not ascending and disjoint, is not maximal (two runs that could be one), or does not cover exactly the checked slots is refused the same way and throws nothing else; a writer's `starts` is canonical (§5.6); a `"UTC"` calendar's snapshot has no `starts`; and, for a record without a provisional tail (every point inside its coverage, as in every page record and every case of §9.1 but j), a snapshot the reader then takes of the same range equals the record field for field, `starts` included. A provisional tail (N35), on `{ unit: "day", zone: "America/New_York", at: "09:30" }` with `finalizedUntil` at the start of 2026-03-06 (14:30 UTC) and bars on 2026-03-05 and 2026-03-06: the snapshot of 2026-03-05 to 2026-03-09 covers the first day and holds both points, so that 2026-03-06 is named by the tail's payload and by nothing else (the range ends on 2026-03-09); `hydrate` into an empty cache of the same config applies the covered day; the reader's read of the range returns the first bar only and misses the slots from 2026-03-06 on; and the reader's snapshot of the range holds one point fewer than the record. The tail's slot is a checked slot (§5.6): with reader's rules that differ on 2026-03-06 only, `hydrate` refuses the whole snapshot, and with rules that differ on 2026-03-07 only, a slot the snapshot does not name, it applies.
14. **The JSON form (N38).** For a calendar cache in a zone and a fixed cache, and a snapshot `s` without a provisional tail (every point inside its coverage): `hydrate(decodeSnapshot(JSON.parse(JSON.stringify(encodeSnapshot(s)))))` into an empty cache of the same config gives the same read as the source, with `NaN` field values preserved; for a snapshot with a tail the decoded value still equals `s`, and the read after `hydrate` lacks the tail's points, as test 13 pins; a decoded format 2 snapshot keeps `format: 2` and `grid` in its envelope and every payload, and with G8 (d) its `starts`, run for run; `decodeSnapshot` refuses with `TscacheError`, and throws nothing else, a format 2 value whose `starts` is not an array (`5`), has a hole, or holds a position that is not an array of exactly three numbers (`[[1, 2]]`, `[[1, 2, "3"]]`), while a value without `starts` decodes and `hydrate` then refuses it for a calendar in a zone other than `"UTC"`; a fixed cache's encoded value is equal, field for field, to 0.1.0's; `decodeSnapshot` refuses an unknown format and a format 2 value whose `grid` is not an object with a string `unit`, with `TscacheError`.
15. **Records.** A calendar cache's records restore into an empty cache whose records are equal again, field for field, `starts` included, which its canonical form determines (§4.10.2's convergence, for calendar caches); a record under another descriptor is not applied and is rewritten; an unknown format is refused.
16. **Fetch request.** It carries the descriptor in `interval` and `alignmentOffset` 0; its range is aligned to slot starts; a miss that abuts covered slots is extended one slot into them (a month on a monthly cache), and a flank with no coverage next to it is not extended.
17. **Tables of slot starts and the cost model (G19, G20).** With a spy on the offset seam counting lookups, every number below being the cost model's (§5.6). Sharing: a table is computed once per resolved descriptor; two caches with equal descriptors share it, two with different ones do not; after reads over more blocks than the capacity, no more tables are held than the capacity; a warm read computes nothing; `clear` and eviction leave the tables; a fixed grid and a UTC calendar never call the seam. The four operations, in `America/New_York` at 09:30 and under injected rules: a G6 resolution makes at most 3 lookups, on 2026-03-08 and 2026-11-01 and at local times that do not exist or exist twice included; a start computed alone makes at most 6, for 2026-03-07 and 2026-03-08 included, and leaves no table; `isAligned`, `slotOf` and `slotAtOrAfter` of one instant make at most 12, for instants on and off the grid on those dates; a batch that needs more than half of a block computes its table once, in at most 3 × 513 lookups, and one that needs half or less computes its starts alone. The inequality: for each record of §5.6's table of measured records at a tenth of its size, for the cases of §9.1 and for random records under injected rules, the lookups of its `hydrate`, of its load as page records and of the `snapshot` that writes it are at most its charge, computed from `count`, `segments.length` and `coverage.length`; a record with one bar in each of 100 distinct cold blocks holds no table afterwards, and a gap of 100,000 slots adds the lookups of its two ends. The budget, lowered through the test seam, with the spy at zero when each refusal happens: `hydrate` refuses a snapshot whose charge exceeds it with `TscacheError` before any lookup, also when its range, its coverage and its payload starts are malformed in ways the later rules would catch; a load whose page records charge more applies the newest pages within the budget, skips the others and makes no lookup for a skipped page; `snapshot` of a range that charges more throws `TscacheError` and never `InvalidRangeError`; and `get(range, { snapshot: true })` of that range resolves with its read and without a `snapshot`.
