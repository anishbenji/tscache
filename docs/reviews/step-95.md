**Why the construction changed.** This area drew findings in the pre-review and in this round, so R1-3 was rebuilt rather than patched. The earlier construction tried to know how many reads the drag made, by counting moves and stops. It no longer depends on that:

- Every request fails from before the drag. Every wait asks one question: do the failed fetches and the held requests together reach from the read of what is on screen through the bar before the opening fetch? That is true however many reads came before and however little each asked for, because a read asks only for what the fetches in flight do not cover (architecture §4.9.1).
- Holding every read of the drag, as round 1 suggested for the wait, failed in testing for a different reason. Chrome opens at most six HTTP/1.1 connections to one host, and the event stream takes one. In two of 90 diagnostic runs the backend held five requests (three history reads and two forming bars, a minute having passed during the drag), and the end read's request waited in the browser and never reached the backend. The diagnostics printed the held ranges: the reads at steps 7, 10 and 16, and none from the end.
- So R1-3 now drags by itself. The first half moves a step at a time and may stop anywhere; its reads fail and come back, holding nothing, and the test stops at steps 7 and 10 until each read is back. Only then are answers held. The rest of the drag is two single moves, to step 17 (a stop) and to the end, which no stop under load can split. At most two reads are held, each a history request and a forming bar: four requests, five connections with the stream.
- The read at step 17 holds the fetch the end read then awaits, so the end read asks for three steps, about 40 minutes. A wait for one request an hour long would never end: the round-1 defect is exercised on every run.
- The shared `drag` helper is back to its form on `main`; the stepped drag with stops lived only in R1-3.
- Not by construction: the page's `showing` output is read for the reach. React writes it in a render after the scroll, so for an instant it can lag the chart. A lag can only make a wait end on an earlier read. After the drag, the end read then asks, after fetches are allowed, for at most the three steps after step 17, about 40 minutes: no history fetch, and it cannot clear the failed ranges it awaits. At the stop at step 10, the read there is then held as well: three reads and, unless two minute boundaries pass during the drag, at most two forming bars: six connections, still within the limit.
